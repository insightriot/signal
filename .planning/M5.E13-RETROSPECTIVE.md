## What changed mid-flight

**`B54`, at PLAN.** Reading `state.js` for FR1.2 turned up `checkGateArtifacts` — an artifact-existence checker nothing called, in the very file FR1.2 proposed to add one to. Worse than inert: executed against Signal it returned `{ready:false, missing:['REQUIREMENTS.md']}`, hardcoding the unprefixed name while Epic mode writes `M5.E13-REQUIREMENTS.md`. **Switching it on would have blocked PLAN for every Epic-mode project.** Four of its five gates were empty arrays behind a comment promising dynamics never implemented. Ratified: **replace**, per M5.E9's remove-don't-re-tune precedent. **D-M5E13-3's locked count of three became four.**

**`B55`, at the last task.** The first real use of the adherence harness — re-running the canary FR1.3 required — returned **INDETERMINATE** (treatment 3/3, control 1/3). Diagnosis: `adherence-run.js` copies the whole plugin and mutates **one** command file, while `transitionPhase` is named 4× each in `plan.md`/`verify.md`/`review.md`. **The control arm has never been isolated across files.** And FR1.1 *amplified it by design* — a single shared wording puts a byte-identical copy of the deleted instruction in the siblings. Homed at **M5.E15**.

**`B46` stopped, not forced.** Its premise — 45 dispositions never stamped back — does not survive measurement: **0 of 48 inbox candidates correspond to any disposition row**. The dispositions' population is the v2 feature-port candidates. Stamping would have written verbs from one population onto another.

**Scope, honestly:** 16 planned tasks; 13 shipped as planned, 1 stopped on evidence, 2 discharged differently, and **two unplanned findings outranked most of the plan**.

## What surprised us

**The Epic's own defect classes appeared in the Epic's own work, five times.**

| # | Where | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | DISCUSS | M5.E8's proposed mechanism covered **1 of its own 3** instances |
| 2 | PLAN | The AC count read **20**; enumeration said **21** — written from the table's shape |
| 3 | VERIFY | The AC-check script tested **20 of 21**, omitting the only failing one, and printed a clean sweep |
| 4 | REVIEW (C1) | `B48` was an **unconditional instruction**; FR1.2 replaced it with an **unconditional guard** |
| 5 | REVIEW (I1) | Two identical literals under a comment asserting *"one definition… which is the divergence `B53` was about"* |

**All five were caught. None by a mechanism** — every one by a human or agent re-deriving a number or re-reading a source that was already written down.

That is the finding. These are not lapses; they are **default behaviour under pressure**, and the pressure was *an Epic explicitly about them*. If they survive that, they survive anything short of automation.

**Second surprise: first execution is where the yield is.** `B55` (first real use of the harness), `B54` (first read of that file), the 2 fired triggers (first walk of the watchlist), `B42`/`B53` (first outside contact), `B48` (first read of a transcript). Not "we worked harder" — *the first time anyone ran a thing instead of assuming it, it was broken.* **"Shipped but never run" is the best defect predictor this codebase has.**

**Third: CI justified itself in four minutes.** Its first run failed — `actions/checkout` shallow-clones, and one test walks real git history. A latent environment dependency nothing had ever stated.

## What we'd do differently

1. **Derive verification from the requirements artifact, not alongside it.** VERIFY's script was assembled from memory of what was built. A check that enumerates its own scope cannot detect a criterion it never listed. → M5.E10.
2. **Schedule first-use in Wave 1.** `B55` surfaced on the last task. Had it surfaced first, FR1.1's shared-wording requirement would have been recognised as *in tension with the measurement* before it was built. → filed.
3. **Enumerate exemptions from the docs, not the plan.** C1 cost a Critical because `execute.md:96` was authoritative and unread.
4. **Don't let a comment stand in for a property.** I1 shipped a duplicated literal under a comment denying duplication. A claim in a comment is not evidence.
5. **Check the full-suite tail before every commit.** One commit was pushed red. Now mechanically impossible — that is what CI is for.

- **`B50`'s class → M5.E16.** The guard class is a **search strategy**, not a bug list. The code-shaped detector is built (2 of 4 instances, labelled); the document-shaped (`B39`'s home) and data-shaped (`B46`'s home) are the open half.

- **`review.md`'s verdict table contradicts practice** — it says *"FAIL — Any Critical"*; M5.E9 and now M5.E13 both shipped PASS-WITH-FIXES with a Critical closed in-phase. One must change.

- **`B46` needs a real remedy.** 48 candidates genuinely resurface at every drain; the cause is not un-stamped dispositions. The tool has only *undecided* or *decided* — no "parked with a review date", which the trigger watchlist already has.

## Anti-rationalization moment

**The moment: the canary came back INDETERMINATE, and re-running it was one command away.**

Three runs per arm, n=3, and the control had split 1-of-3. A second run would very likely have produced 0/3 and a clean `OBEYED` — restoring the headline verdict, closing FR1.3 green, and letting the Epic ship with its measurement story intact. Nobody would have known. The harness even prints the exact number of runs, so a re-run looks like diligence.

**It was not re-run.** Because M5.E8 had already written the rule down — the four-verdict impostor table exists precisely to stop a pre-authorised acceptable outcome being reached by repetition — and because the split was *evidence*, not noise. Chasing it produced `B55`, which is worth more than the verdict it replaced.

**The second moment, smaller and closer to the bone:** REVIEW's Critical (C1) was found by asking *"does this guard break a tier we never run?"* — a question with no acceptance criterion behind it, at the end of a long Epic, with 21/21 criteria already green and every incentive to stop. `B42` is what happens when nobody asks it: a P1 alive for nine releases.

**The rationalization I actually caught myself in:** writing VERIFY's report, I had "20 PASS" on screen and a clean narrative. The only reason AC2.2 surfaced is that I compared the count to VALIDATION's stated 21. **The pull was not to lie — it was to stop counting.**

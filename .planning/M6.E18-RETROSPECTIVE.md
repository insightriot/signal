# M6.E18 — Retrospective: the review loop's signals become trustworthy

**Label:** *"Make the review loop's signals trustworthy, then prove them with a driven Epic"*.
Priority 1 of [`BACKLOG-REVIEW-2026-10-20.md`](BACKLOG-REVIEW-2026-10-20.md), picked by Brett. FULL
tier, `attention: checkpointed`, driven by `/sig:drive` from DISCUSS. Every signal the review loop
stops on was made to say what happened: the PR reviewer's verdict (it reviewed only a PR's first push;
a `review-head` label now asks for a review of the head commit, and SHIP will not call a PR ready to
merge without one), the loop ceiling (a visit marker, so an earlier visit's PASS no longer lifts it),
a pass record (`recordPass` refuses what cannot be true), a fix round's proof (`checkFixRounds` asks
git), and SHIP's "what is left" (`/sig:resume` names the merge and the confirm instead of "done").

## What assumptions broke

- **"The reviewer passed without reviewing."** It did exactly what its command says. The filed
  diagnosis was the shape of the symptom; the transcript was the artifact.
- **"A same-phase re-entry needs a smarter rule."** Three rounds each made it smarter and each opened
  a new hole (an earlier PASS lifting the ceiling; restarts never counted; a crash wedging the phase).
  The simple rule — every entry is a new visit — can only stop the ceiling sooner.
- **"A fix round's proof is the round's own."** Round 1's parent run had 13 pins that passed on the
  base; the tool counted them and the round's record states them as a gap, not proof.

## What surprised us

- **The PR reviewer had reviewed one of #304's ten pushes.** The green tick said nothing about which.
- **The security auditor found a forgery route this Epic opened**: a PR that edits the reviewer
  workflow runs its own copy and could print `REVIEW_OUTCOME=reviewed`. Such a PR now reads
  cannot-check — which is also simply true.
- **This Epic's REVIEW report quoted `<!--` inline while describing I4** — the exact text I4 is about.
- **`checkFixRounds`' first real use with git** accepted pass 2 and pass 3: every parent-run commit
  resolved to its range's base and the five rounds chained without a gap.

## What we'd do differently

- **Decide a rule's meaning before coding its fix.** The re-entry rule was patched round by round
  from each read's finding; nobody wrote down what a "visit" is first. A design question in a fix
  round is a sign to stop the round and ask.
- **Ship the workflow PR on day one and get it merged early.** #306 waited the whole run, so this
  Epic's own PR could not be reviewed at its head until SHIP.
- **Give executors the fixture's real shape.** Two agents' tests assumed an empty phase log; the
  shared `writeState` fixture has entries.

- **The driven-Epic proof the M6.E17 retro asked for happened**: `/sig:drive` advanced DISCUSS →

  records rather than from prose.

## Anti-rationalization moment

At pass 2's third round the read found a Critical in the round's own fix. A fourth round was one
small change away — skip the artifact check on a re-entry, count only PASS as "recorded" — and the
fixes were each a few lines. It was not taken: the ceiling is three rounds, and three rounds in a row
had each been "a few lines" that opened the next hole. The question went to Brett with a revert as
the recommendation, and the revert's two narrow reads came back clean.

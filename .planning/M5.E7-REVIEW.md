## §0 — Scope of this review, and what it deliberately did not do

**Steps 2, 3, and 4 of `commands/review.md` did not run.** `review_depth: quality-only` is the
master switch (preamble precedence rule): security hardening, performance analysis, and the
simplification pass are skipped **regardless** of the other flags. Here that is substantive rather
than procedural — the deliverable is markdown, so there is no attack surface, no hot path, and no
code to simplify. `M5.E7-PROFILE.md` sets `security_audit: none`, `performance_pass: false`,
`simplification_pass: false` for the same reason (D-M5E7-2, *"strict thinking, zero test theater"*).

**No specialist agents were spawned.** Prior Epics ran 3-specialist adversarial panels; that was a
choice, not a requirement of the command, and this session's operating rules forbid it. **What was
substituted matters more than the count:** §6.1's own charge is *"run by the same pair that built the
thing."* A review inside the same session that read the artifacts **is that risk, not a cure for
it.** So every load-bearing claim below was **re-derived from the primary source** rather than from
the roadmap's summary of it — `M5.E7-COUNTERFACTUAL.md` §3.1, `.planning/BACKLOG.md`,
`.planning/DECISIONS.md` D-M5E7-1…12, `M5.E7-DISPOSITIONS.md` §6–§8.5. **This does not close the gap
and is not claimed to.** A genuinely independent reviewer is *itself a build item* — M5.E8(b),
the second-opinion replay — and this review is one more instance of the need for it.

**VERIFY's 22 mechanical re-derivations were not repeated.** REVIEW attacked what VERIFY explicitly
routed here: the four claims §4 of `M5.E7-VERIFICATION.md` marked as *"where the Epic fails if it
fails"* — roadmap §6 and dispositions §6.

## Important Issues (fixed in-phase)

### I1 — §2.1 claims a universal the corpus does not support, and `BACKLOG.md` contradicted itself 25 lines apart

**The claim.** §2.1: *"In every case the knowledge was in-context at the moment of the miss. **Nobody
forgot anything**, so a cross-session store with confidence decay prevents none of it."* Immediately
followed by *"Consequence: the entire COMPOUND port group is cut."*

**Two defects, and the second is worse than the first.**

1. **It is scoped to three chains but reads as universal.** The roadmap's own §5 concedes *"Signal's
   one genuine cross-session recurrence (`B13`'s NUL byte)"* — 200 lines later, in a table cell, in
   the row that cuts the thing. `M5.E7-COUNTERFACTUAL.md` §3.1 states it plainly at source
   (*"Its one genuine failure was grep-blindness (B13's recurrence)"*). **A reader of §2.1 alone gets
   "nobody forgot anything"; a reader of §5 gets one documented instance of exactly that.**
2. **In `.planning/BACKLOG.md` — the *enforced* doc — the two sat 25 lines apart in the same
   section.** § Sprint 4's header said *"Nobody forgot anything"*; the `/retro` + `/learn` row below
   it said *"Signal's one genuine cross-session recurrence (`B13`'s NUL byte, **learned 2026-07-18,
   violated 2026-07-25**)"*. Learned and violated **seven days apart** is the definition of the thing
   the header denies.

**And the survivorship argument §6.6 makes was never applied where it bites hardest.** §6.6 argues
the bug ledger only records defects Signal's process was good enough to find, then routes that to
M5.E8(b). The identical logic applies to §2.1 and is absent: **the three chains were selected
*because* they are documented**, so a miss caused by genuine forgetting that nobody ever caught is
missing from the corpus by construction. Three in-context chains cannot establish "nobody forgot
anything" — they establish "the misses we can see were in-context."

**Does the cut survive? Yes — and that is why this is Important, not Critical.** `B13` is cut on an
**independent and stronger** ground: its real defence is a deterministic content check
`doc-hygiene.js` already hosts, and gstack's decay-filtered digest fires at skill start in 10 of 54
skills, which would not plausibly have surfaced *"don't paste control bytes"* at the moment someone
edited a bug entry. **The COMPOUND cut needs no rescue; the claim needed its true scope.**

**Fix applied** — `analysis/SIGNAL-V2-ROADMAP.md` §2.1 and `.planning/BACKLOG.md` § Sprint 4:
re-scoped to *"in all three documented chains,"* `B13` named **at the point of the claim** with its
separate grounds, and the selection-bias limit stated with a pointer to §6.6. **No verb changed.**

### I3 — The falsifier had no enforceable home, and structurally cannot ride a trigger

§6.1's falsifier is the roadmap's whole answer to the confirmation-bias charge: *"M5.E8 lands,
measurement shows Signal's enforcement is as good as claimed, and the ports still never happen. If

condition.

**Fix applied** — landed in `.planning/BACKLOG.md` under **M5.E9** as a dated check: **2026-10-26**,
with three permitted written verdicts (promote / re-park with a *new* date / **record the reframe as

## §7 — New bug cataloged at REVIEW: `B41` (`confirmed`, P2)

Found while checking this phase's own STATE write, and **triaged by reading the code before being
labelled** (grep over `commands/*.md`; `transitionPhase` at `tools/lib/state.js:376`).

**`transitionPhase` is called by exactly three commands** — `calibrate.md`, `discuss.md`,
`ship.md`. **`plan.md`, `execute.md`, `verify.md`, and `review.md` never call it and instruct no
other phase write.** In a **command-driven** project `/sig:discuss` sets `phase: DISCUSS` and
nothing moves it again until `/sig:ship` appends whatever `phase` still says — ending at
`completed_phases: [DISCUSS]`, `phase: SHIP`, with **PLAN / EXECUTE / VERIFY / REVIEW never recorded
at all** while `/sig:status` and `/sig:resume` report `DISCUSS` for the whole build. The symptom is
already in the code as an **unsatisfiable precondition**: `plan.md:50` says *"verify current phase is
PLAN"* and no command can make that true.

**The aggravator is what makes it P2 rather than cosmetic.** `plan.md:149`, `verify.md:72`, and
`review.md` Step 5b all call `markFresh`, whose stated purpose is *"so the staleness banner reads as
fresh after {PHASE} closes."* Each therefore **stamps a fresh timestamp over a stale position**,
converting *stale-and-flagged* into **stale-and-silent** — it disables the one signal that would
surface the drift. **Live instance today:** after this phase's Step 5b `markFresh`, STATE read
`phase: VERIFY` with a fresh stamp; no banner would have fired. **Caught by hand, not by anything.**

**Masked on Signal-on-Signal** because this repo maintains `phase`/`completed_phases` by hand —
which is how it survived 11 releases unnoticed.

**Homed in M5.E9, not M5.E8, and the reason is the roadmap's own rule:** this is a **deterministic,
file-shaped** defect — a check either runs or it doesn't — so §1's sorting rule says build it now
rather than waiting on measurement. *It is also a small instance of this Epic's headline finding
wearing different clothes: a state ledger nothing checks.*

**Corrected in-phase for this repo:** `transitionPhase(baseDir, 'REVIEW')` was called after the fix
commit, so STATE now reads `phase: REVIEW` with `VERIFY (2026-07-26)` recorded. **That fixes the
data, not the defect** — `B41` tracks the defect.

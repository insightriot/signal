## Observations for S1.t3 (not conclusions)

**Specificity holds up.** Roughly 60% are direct "add X to Y"; ~25% promote-an-existing-practice
to a default (still a product change); ~15% are ledger pointers. **Vague reflections: 0 of 39.**
The template's own phrasing forces a named target.

**Theme F is the standout gap.** Items **17, 29, 32** (+35 adjacent) — EXECUTE delegation and
dispatch guidance, raised independently in M5.E1, M5.E4, M5.E5, with **zero coverage in any of the
four ledgers** (`delegat|executor cost` → 0 hits). Three consecutive Epics, never tracked.

**Theme A is the most-repeated endorsement** (11, 18, 20, 24) — the adversarial REVIEW panel is
asked *four separate times* to become a **default**, not a per-Epic choice. That is a
promote-to-default demand, and it is cultural/untracked rather than ticketed.

**Theme B is the largest cluster** (11, 12, 17, 19, 21, 22, 23, 26, 27) — nine items on
green-tests≠proven. This is Signal's most-felt pain and its most-repeated lesson.

**Self-reference worth flagging to t3:** item 33 (B36) names *"the next retro/compound Epic"* as
its home — the corpus is pointing at M5.E7's likely conclusion from inside the evidence. Handle
under AC7.2: that is a reason for **more** adversarial scrutiny of the memory-loop thesis, not less.

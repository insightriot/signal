## 2. The central finding: the flat rule splits one slice into two units

**This is the Epic's real problem, and it is not the problem the requirements describe.**

`eval-project-A` writes PLAN-phase artifacts as `PLAN-{unit}-{ARTIFACT}.md` and execution artifacts as
`{unit}-{ARTIFACT}.md`. Right-anchored matching therefore produces **two units for one slice**:

| Files | Derived unit |
|---|---|
| `PLAN-GATE-A-RESEARCH.md`, `PLAN-GATE-A-VALIDATION.md` | `PLAN-GATE-A` |
| `GATE-A-PROGRESS.md` | `GATE-A` |
| `PLAN-SLICE-SSO-RESEARCH.md`, `PLAN-SLICE-SSO-VALIDATION.md` | `PLAN-SLICE-SSO` |
| `SLICE-SSO-PROGRESS.md`, `SLICE-SSO-REVIEW.md`, `SLICE-SSO-VERIFICATION.md` | `SLICE-SSO` |

**Verified as one slice, not two badly-named ones** — read, not inferred:

- `PLAN-GATE-A.md` opens `# Gate A — PLAN (2026-07-29)`, and `GATE-A-PROGRESS.md` opens
  `# Gate A — execution progress` and links **`Plan: [PLAN-GATE-A.md]`** directly.
- `PLAN-SLICE-SSO.md` opens `# PLAN — Slice SSO`; `SLICE-SSO-VERIFICATION.md` opens
  `# Slice SSO — VERIFICATION`.

**Why this breaks FR2 as written.** The terminal artifact and the verdict land on one side of the
split; the plan and research land on the other. Applying FR2 to `SLICE-SSO`: it has a
`VERIFICATION`, it is not the current unit (`current_epic: null`), and its verdict is readable and
passing → **`closed` → archived.** `PLAN-SLICE-SSO` has no terminal artifact → **`open` → stays.**

> **The Epic would archive half a slice and leave the other half in root.** That is worse than the
> status quo it exists to fix: today eval-project-A is archived by nothing, which is at least coherent.

The same-unit pairs are `GATE-A`/`PLAN-GATE-A`, `SC1`/`PLAN-SC1`, `SLICE-SSO`/`PLAN-SLICE-SSO`,
`VOICE1`/`PLAN-SLICE-VOICE1` — **4 of eval-project-A's 13 derived units are one half of a split pair, so
8 of 13 units are really 4 slices.**

**This makes AC1.2 wrong as specified.** AC1.2 asserts `PLAN-GATE-A-RESEARCH.md` → `PLAN-GATE-A` and
frames it as the *desired* behaviour (*"a unit name that begins with an artifact word is not
mis-split"*). Measured, that behaviour **is the defect**. The rule reads the leading `PLAN-` as part
of the name when it is in fact a phase marker. `PLAN-GATE-A` and `GATE-A` must resolve to **one**
unit, or FR2 must refuse to close either.

**This is a scope change, not a typo — it needs Brett's call** (see §6).

## 4. The suffix list: generating names is not parsing them

**FR1's premise is that the list is "declared in one place and is the same list the doc-runtime
already uses."** That premise does not survive.

`SCAFFOLD_SUFFIXES` (`archive-tree.js:52`) is the only such list — 8 entries: `REQUIREMENTS`,
`RESEARCH`, `PLAN`, `PROGRESS`, `VERIFICATION`, `VALIDATION`, `REVIEW`, `SHIP`. **`RETROSPECTIVE` is
deliberately excluded**, documented in the comment above it: retros stay in root as the warm
traceability spine.

But it was built for the **opposite job**. `planArchiveMoves` (`:96-110`) does not parse filenames at
all — it takes `closedEpicIds` and **constructs** `{epicId}-{suffix}.md`, then checks presence. It is
generate-and-check. FR1 needs parse-and-group. **A list that is complete for generating Signal's own
names is not complete for parsing everyone else's**, which is exactly what AC1.4 hit.

**Real trailing-token distribution, all 12 trees** (`| awk -F- '{print $NF}' | sort | uniq -c`):

```
35 RESEARCH   35 PLAN        34 VALIDATION   28 VERIFICATION   28 PROGRESS
27 REVIEW     27 RETROSPECTIVE  23 REQUIREMENTS  14 PROFILE     12 STATE
12 PROJECT    11 CONTEXT     6 LANDSCAPE     5 QUESTIONS       5 INDEX
5 IDEAS       4 BUGS         3 SHIP          3 RUNBOOK         3 HISTORY
3 DECISIONS   2 SCOPE  2 REPORT  2 BRIEF  2 DATA  2 DECISION  2 ARCHIVE …
```

The 8 declared suffixes are the top 8 by frequency *except* `RETROSPECTIVE` (27, excluded by design)
and `PROFILE` (14 — Signal's own `{EpicID}-PROFILE.md`, currently ungrouped). The tail — `RUNBOOK`,
`SCOPE`, `REPORT`, `BRIEF` — is where AC1.4 broke.

**PLAN must decide, with a reason:** widen the list, treat the tail as `ungrouped` and accept that
some units under-form, or derive membership some other way. Widening is not free — `PROFILE`,
`STATE`, `PROJECT`, `CONTEXT`, `INDEX`, `BUGS` are **root singletons** that must never form units,
so any widening needs a bare-name exclusion (`PROFILE.md` ≠ unit `PROFILE`).

## 5. Other findings

### 5.1 Phase names are not unit names (`eval-project-E`, `eval-project-F`)

`eval-project-E` derives units `PLAN`, `REVIEW`, `VERIFY` from `PLAN-PLAN.md`, `PLAN-PROGRESS.md`,
`PLAN-RESEARCH.md`, `PLAN-VALIDATION.md`, `REVIEW-REVIEW.md`, `VERIFY-VERIFICATION.md`. `eval-project-F`
derives `EXECUTE`, `PLAN` the same way.

These are `resolveArtifactPath`'s **pattern 3** — the literal-substitution `{PHASE}-{ARTIFACT}.md`
form for linear projects. They are phase artifacts of one linear project, **not units of work**.
Archiving "unit `PLAN`" would archive the project's live plan.

**Required, and absent from the requirements: the seven canonical phase names must be excluded as
unit names.** New AC.

### 5.2 Already-archived units derive nothing — idempotency comes free

13 Signal units (`M4.5.E1`–`M4.5.E11`, `M5.E1`, `M5.E2`) and 2 in `eval-project-F` have a retrospective in
root and **no other top-level artifact**. Verified cause: their scaffolds are already archived —
`.planning/archive/M4.5/E1/` holds `M4.5.E1-PLAN.md`, `-PROGRESS.md`, `-RESEARCH.md`,
`-VALIDATION.md`. That is `SCAFFOLD_SUFFIXES`' documented design working correctly.

**Consequence for PLAN open question 2 ("stable across runs"):** because derivation reads only the
top level and archived files have left it, **a second dry-run proposes nothing by construction.** The
stability requirement is satisfied by the derivation's shape, not by extra bookkeeping — provided the
archive destination is outside the scanned set.

**Consequence for FR4:** these units are invisible to derivation while being fully closed. "0 units
to archive" on Signal's own tree must not render as *nothing has ever been archived*.

### 5.3 Signal's own tree contains a non-Epic unit

`v0.1.6` — `v0.1.6-PLAN.md`, `-PROGRESS.md`, `-REQUIREMENTS.md`, `-RESEARCH.md`, `-RETROSPECTIVE.md`,
`-REVIEW.md`, `-VALIDATION.md`, `-VERIFICATION.md`. Eight artifacts, a real closed unit, named after
a release rather than an Epic — so **`EPIC_ID_STRICT_RE` rejects it and Signal's own archive path
cannot reach it.** The Epic's thesis is demonstrable without leaving this repo.

### 5.4 Verdict formats vary more than FR2 assumes

Four real formats found, all at the top of their files:

- `**Verdict: PASS.**` — `eval-project-C` `PHASE8-VERIFICATION.md:3`
- `**Verdict: ✅ PASS**` — `eval-project-I` `M1-VERIFICATION.md:5` (emoji inside the bold)
- `**Verdict:** ✅ **PASS (structural) — with the real-Entra-walk exit gate (NFR-SSO.5) explicitly
  DEFERRED as externally-gated.**` — `eval-project-A` `SLICE-SSO-VERIFICATION.md:3`
- Prose restatement later in the same file: `Verdict remains **PASS**` —
  `eval-project-I` `M1-VERIFICATION.md:83`

**Two hazards the requirements do not name.** First, **multiple verdict mentions per file** — a
first-match and a last-match parser can disagree, and nothing says which is authoritative. Second,
and sharper: eval-project-A's verdict says **PASS** while the same sentence records the slice's FULL exit
gate as **OPEN**. A substring match on `PASS` would close a unit whose own verification says it is
not finished. FR2's *"where a verdict line is readable, it passed"* is doing more work than one
`/PASS/` test can carry.

### 5.5 Nested `.planning/` content is out of scope but not out of the way

6 of 12 projects hold substantial nested `.md` (eval-project-A 424, eval-project-D 289, eval-project-F 98,
signal 95, eval-project-C 73, eval-project-J 21), under `archive/`, `research/`, `scan/`, `phases/`,
`.curator/`. FR1 says `.planning/*.md`. **PLAN should state the top-level-only scope explicitly** so
the dry-run's counts are not read as whole-tree counts — and so `archive/` is never rescanned.

### 5.6 NFR4 — path safety

Across all 12 trees, **no derived unit name contains `..`, a separator, or a leading dot.** Longest
is `PLAN-SLICE-VOICE1` (17 chars). Units containing a dot are all Epic IDs (`M5.E16`, `M2.8.E1`).

**But the claim that this is the first untrusted input holds.** Every name reaching the archive path
today passed `EPIC_ID_STRICT_RE` (`M{n}[.{n}]*.E{n}`), which cannot express a traversal. FR1's names
come from arbitrary filenames on disk, so `path-confine.js` receives genuinely
attacker-influenceable input **for the first time**. Clean today ≠ safe by construction.

### 9.1 A unit-derivation-from-filenames implementation **already exists**

```js
// tools/lib/state-drift.js:421
const WORKED_UNIT_IN_FILENAME = /^(.+?)-(PLAN|PROGRESS|VERIFICATION|REVIEW)\.md$/;
```

Written by **M5.E16's `C1` fix**, for this exact problem, and paired in `run` with a **categorical
exclusion of the seven phase names** (`:463-468`) — including the reasoning FR1 would have had to
rediscover: *"A PHASE NAME is not a unit of work … Excluding the seven phase names is CATEGORICAL (a
closed, enumerated set), not a threshold."*

**This changes PLAN open question 1.** The question asks whether FR1 lives in a new module or widens
`archive-tree.js`, naming `evict.js` and `retro-index.js` as the other strict-ID holders. **It missed
a third site that already implements the answer.** Building FR1 in `archive-tree.js` would create the
second implementation of one rule that the question itself warns against, and that M5.E13 REVIEW
flagged as a defect class.

Differences to reconcile, not ignore: the existing regex carries **4** suffixes where
`SCAFFOLD_SUFFIXES` has 8, and its lazy `(.+?)` is equivalent to a right-anchored longest-suffix
match only because of the `$` anchor — that equivalence should be pinned by a test, not assumed.

**Recommendation: extract the regex + the phase-name exclusion into one shared module that
`state-drift.js` and the new archive planner both consume.** Evidenced single-home answer. §5.1's
"new AC needed" is withdrawn — the rule exists; it needs relocating, not inventing.

### 9.2 `isEpicDone` has exactly one caller — and `senseArchiveTree` is not it

```
tools/lib/retrospective.js:168   definition
commands/discuss.md:54           the ONLY caller — the done-Epic guard
```

**AC5.3 resolves cleanly:** tightening `isEpicDone` *is* tightening `/sig:discuss`'s guard, because
there is nowhere else it reaches. The AC's "either it gains the condition or its callers do" is a
choice between two spellings of one change.

**But FR5 is two fixes, not one.** `senseArchiveTree` does **not** route through `isEpicDone`:

```js
// tools/lib/archive-tree.js:357
const closedEpicIds = retros.map((r) => r.epicId);   // isStub computed upstream, discarded here
```

**CONFIRMED — no `isStub` filter.** So the archive path and the discuss guard prove closure by two
independent mechanisms that share one bug (*a file existing is not a finished unit*). Fixing one
leaves the other. **This is the "fix by class, then search for siblings" discipline STATE.md asks
for, with the siblings already located.**

### 9.3 NEW DEFECT — the done-Epic guard cannot fire for a linear project

```js
// tools/lib/retrospective.js:169
if (typeof epicId !== 'string' || !EPIC_ID_STRICT_RE.test(epicId)) return false;
```

`isEpicDone` is itself strict-gated, and returns **`false`** — *"not done"* — for any non-strict ID.
So on `eval-project-C` (`current_epic: PHASE12`), `/sig:discuss`'s guard evaluates to *not done* and
**never halts**, whatever the state of `PHASE12`'s artifacts. The guard's own docblock states its
purpose: *"a writing command against a done Epic with no `--epic` must halt rather than clobber that
Epic's artifacts."*

**A linear project can re-run DISCUSS into a completed unit and clobber its `REQUIREMENTS.md`, and
the guard built to prevent that returns `false` by construction.** This is `B42`'s shape — a
documented mode a whole guard silently does not serve — and it is **not in M5.E18's requirements**.
Filing recommended; homing is Brett's call (it is squarely in this Epic's territory).

### 9.4 `EPIC_ID_STRICT_RE` — 15 call sites, three different failure modes

Definition: `state.js:109` — `/^M\d+(\.\d+)*\.E\d+$/`.

| Behaviour on non-match | Sites |
|---|---|
| **Throws** | `retrospective.js:147` (`deriveRetroPath`), `state.js:888` (`setCurrentEpic`) |
| **Returns false / empty / null** — caller cannot tell "no match" from "matched, answer is no" | `retrospective.js:169` (`isEpicDone`), `state.js:965` (`detectMode` → `'linear'`), `profile.js:318`, `resume.js:158`, `status.js:249` |
| **Silently filters out** | `archive-tree.js:102` (`planArchiveMoves`), `evict.js:175`, `migrate-memory.js:818` |
| **Explicitly reports blindness** — the good pattern | `state-drift.js:623, 649, 716` (returns `APPLICABILITY.BLIND` with a reason) |

**The fourth row is the model FR3 should follow, and it already exists in this codebase.**
`state-drift.js` distinguishes *could not look* from *looked and found nothing*; the other eleven
sites conflate them. That conflation is the Epic's whole subject.

### 9.5 FR6 — real, latent, and currently unobservable

```js
// tools/lib/state-drift.js:449-450
applicability: ({ files }) => files.some((f) => f.endsWith('-RETROSPECTIVE.md')) ? EVAL : NA
```

**CONFIRMED** — the probe keys on the hyphen-prefixed form, so a project whose only retro is an
unprefixed `RETROSPECTIVE.md` reports `not-applicable`.

**But measured against the corpus, the blast radius today is 0 of 12.** Exactly one project has an

FR6 is still worth fixing — it fails safe today only by luck of file inventory, not by design — but
**the Epic must not claim it fixes an observed problem.** Stating "0 live instances" is the same

## 10. AC2.2 re-measured: `cannotDetermine` is the common path, not the edge case

§3 recorded that AC2.2's two named fixtures do not support it. Replacing them meant scanning every
terminal artifact on the corpus rather than picking two more by hand.

**31 terminal artifacts** (`*VERIFICATION.md` + `*SHIP.md`, top level, 12 trees).
**11 of 31 — 35% — carry no first-line-parseable pass/fail verdict**, in three distinct shapes:

**Shape 1 — no verdict mention at all (4):**
`eval-project-C/PHASE8-SHIP.md`, `PHASE10-SHIP.md`, `PHASE11-SHIP.md`, `eval-project-L/T23-VERIFICATION.md`

**Shape 2 — "Verdict" is a section heading; the value lives in the body below it (6):**
`eval-project-E/VERIFY-VERIFICATION.md` (`## Verdict`), `eval-project-K/R1-VERIFICATION.md`
(`## Verdict`), `eval-project-L/T24` (`## 5. Verdict`), `eval-project-L/T26` (`## 6. Verdict`),
`eval-project-J/3-VERIFICATION.md` (`## VERIFY phase verdict`), `eval-project-A/SC1-VERIFICATION.md`
(`## Verdict — **one gate left, and it needs a browser**`)

**Shape 3 — a different *kind* of verdict matches first (1), and it is a trap:**
`eval-project-L/T25-VERIFICATION.md` leads with `**Nyquist verdict: STRICT-clean.**` — a coverage verdict,
not a phase outcome. A first-match parser reads `STRICT-clean` as the unit's result. The phase
verdict, if any, is elsewhere in the file.

### What this changes

1. **AC2.2 now has real fixtures.** Recommend `eval-project-C/PHASE8-SHIP.md` (shape 1) and
   `eval-project-L/T25-VERIFICATION.md` (shape 3, the trap). Shape 2 deserves its own AC — a heading is
   not a value, and six files rely on that distinction.
2. **FR2's `cannotDetermine` is the majority outcome for verdict-reading, not a safety valve.** DISCUSS
   framed it as the branch for the odd malformed file. It is 35% of the corpus. FR4's four-status
   reporting therefore carries the release, exactly as it did in M5.E16.
3. **FR2 does not say which artifact's verdict wins when a unit has several.**
   `eval-project-C`'s `PHASE8` has **both** `PHASE8-VERIFICATION.md` (`Verdict: PASS`, AC2.3's
   fixture) **and** `PHASE8-SHIP.md` (no verdict at all). FR2(a) accepts *"a `VERIFICATION` **or**
   `SHIP` artifact"* without ranking them. Under one reading `PHASE8` is `closed`; under another it
   is `cannotDetermine`. **PLAN must rank them and say why** — a gap the requirements do not name.
4. **`eval-project-I/M1-VERIFICATION.md` is the multiple-verdict case** (§5.4): parseable `PASS` at
   line 5, prose `Verdict remains **PASS**` at line 83. Both agree here, so it is a latent rather
   than live hazard — but it pins that first-match vs last-match must be a stated rule, not an
   accident of implementation.

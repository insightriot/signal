## What changed mid-flight

- **The design of "finished" changed twice, both times by Brett.** DISCUSS said finished markers become
  legacy closes (`D-M6E15-3`, `-10`, `-18`). After two failed reviews it became wording + resolving
  evidence (`-24`); after a third, a proposal the person confirms (`-25`). AC4.1/AC4.2 were amended at
  REVIEW and AC4.3 added; the denominator moved from 29 to 30, stated in VERIFICATION.
- **The loop ceiling was passed on purpose.** Fix loop 3, VERIFY 4 and REVIEW 4 ran past it on Brett's
  explicit choice at the pass-3 halt, recorded in the REVIEW report. VERIFY pass 3 also ran after the
  drive gate reported `loop-ceiling`, on Brett's earlier "the remainder in the recommended order"
  — recorded in VERIFICATION.
- **`SIG-292` was fixed instead of filed.** The loop-2 executor found that a folded `<details>` block
  could lend its wording and references to the row above. It was the Critical's own class, so it was
  fixed before review rather than parked; the test showed it was worse than reported (the fold's own
  "Done" closed the live row).

## What assumptions broke

- **"A deny-list of qualifier words, made long enough, closes the class."** Three reviews each found
  new phrasings. Even the allow-list grammar of loop 2 hid a deny-list behind the dash. What ended it
  was not a better rule but a different contract: nothing closes unseen.
- **"A resolving reference proves the entry is done."** It proves a cited change exists — a PR number
  in a grouping heading, a cross-reference to another backlog row ("split from #99"), or a commit that
  *introduced* the bug all resolved. Evidence narrows what is proposed; the person's yes is the check.
- **"Default branch" and `HEAD` are the same thing.** Written as one in `D-M6E15-25`'s own text; the
  pass-4 reviewer found the code read the checked-out branch, and a scratch clone of a working folder
  showed `origin/HEAD` can name a feature branch.

## What surprised us

- **Every review found the Critical class again, and every fix of it was green.** AC4.2 passed VERIFY
  each time because the fixtures held only the phrasings someone had already thought of. Mutation
  testing (pass 3: 30 of 36 word-deletion mutants survived) showed the tests checked the list of known
  phrases, not the rule.
- **The safety change shrank the review.** Once a wrong proposal is visible instead of silent, the same
  leaking phrase drops from Critical to Suggestion — pass 4 found 0 Critical where passes 1–3 found the
  class each time.
- **Invisible Unicode tag characters became a security finding only because the confirm step is new:**
  text a person cannot see but a model reads, in the one place a person approves.
- **An executor's `git stash pop` applied an old, unrelated stash** onto the tree; it restored the files
  before committing, and said so.

## What we'd do differently

- **Ask "what if the rule is wrong?" at DISCUSS for any rule that changes status silently.** The
  confirm step was available from day one and cheaper than three review loops.
- **Mutation-test a rule's tests before calling it verified**, not at REVIEW. VERIFY's green AC4.2 was
  recorded as wrong twice.
- **Write the requirement's words into the code literally** ("default branch") and test the case where
  they differ from the convenient reading.

## Anti-rationalization moment

After REVIEW pass 2, the leak was a handful of phrasings and another word list would have turned the
listed cases green — the same move that had already failed twice. The pass-3 reviewers called the
residue a design flaw, not a gap, and the honest answer at the ceiling was to put the choice to Brett
rather than run a fourth word list past it. At pass 4 the in-phase fix fitted in 49 lines; the six-line
comment correction it had left stale was made and disclosed rather than skipped to stay under the cap.

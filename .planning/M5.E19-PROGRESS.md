### The S1 finding — refused-as-open units are invisible

**Four units were considered. The output names one.**

`GATE-B` (`cannotDetermine`) is reported. But `M1.E2`, `M1.E3` and `SLICE-BILLING` — all
**considered and refused** — appear nowhere. A reader sees *"4 files + 5 files will move"* and
cannot tell whether the other units were **examined and declined** or **never looked at**.

That is this repo's standing defect shape (`B39`) sitting in the reporting layer, and the
**stub-veto case is the sharpest instance**: `M1.E2` is reported `closed` by `resolveClosures` and
then refused one layer later because its retrospective is `[FILL IN]`. Today that refusal is
**completely silent** — the single most surprising thing the command does, and it says nothing.

`explainArchiveOutcome` is not wrong; its one consumer is `/sig:migrate-memory`'s dry-run, where the
question is *"why is this count what it is?"* rather than *"what did you decide about each unit?"*
A command whose entire job is archiving owes the second answer.

**Consequence for the plan:** S2/S3 are **not** a thin wrapper — `refusals` must carry `open` and
vetoed units, which nothing currently surfaces. S1 shrank nothing; it found the actual gap. Had the
renderer been written first, it would have rendered the same blind spot with new code.

### Bonus: what the sandbox proved about itself

`ungrouped` is `PROFILE.md`, `STATE.md`, and the **two retrospectives** — correct, because
`SCAFFOLD_SUFFIXES` deliberately excludes `RETROSPECTIVE` (retros stay in root as the traceability
spine). A reader seeing *"4 ungrouped"* should not read it as a defect, so the renderer must label
what ungrouped **means**, not just count it.

## What this could not establish

- **That an installed rule behaves as expected.** Every test here verifies what Signal *proposes*.
  Whether Claude Code's matcher treats `Bash(git add:*)` and `Bash(git add *)` as equivalent was
  never tested, and the outcome measurement's agreement figure assumes they are.
- **That the proposal is *good*.** 34 criteria verify that it is derived, classified, bounded and
  honestly labelled. None of them verify that the resulting rule set is the right one for anybody.
  The outcome measurement is the only evidence on that question, its sample is `n = 1`, and that one
  machine belongs to the person who designed the flow.
- **Anything about the prompt layer's behaviour.** `commands/permissions.md` is prose. Nothing fails
  if a session ignores it — the `B75` shape, present here as it is everywhere, and the reason the
  load-bearing checks were put in JS.
- **The three `FR3.2` doc-integrity assertions compare tokens, not meaning.** They pin that
  `stack-detect.js` *says* it is a new detector and not a reuse. They cannot pin that the statement
  stays true if `/sig:init` later grows one.
- **`AC5.2d` proves nothing reads `.planning/PERMISSIONS.md` by that name today.** It is a grep. It
  cannot prove nothing ever will, and it would not catch a reader that resolved the path indirectly.
- **The extractor's residual hole is unmeasured outside this corpus.** An unknown binary written as
  `binary subcommand` with no flag is not detected; measured zero instances *here*, and no other
  payload was scanned.
- **No corpus run.** Every check ran against this repository, synthetic fixtures, and
  `examples/url-shortener`. The eval corpus was not swept, so the reach of `/sig:permissions` across
  real projects of other shapes is **unknown, not clean**.

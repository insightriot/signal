# M6.E9 — Retrospective

**Shipped:** every one of Signal's 26 agents carries a determination. 7 dispatched, 19 dormant with a
reason and a trigger, 0 cut. `plan.md` stopped ordering runs to spawn three agents that never
existed. Five guards now fail the suite on an undetermined agent, a false dispatch claim, a
double-listing, a missing trigger, or a missing reason. **3347 → 3354 tests.**

## The finding, and it is not the roster

**Six times this Epic, something disagreed with the roster page. Every single time, the CHECK was
wrong and the page was right.**

| # | Phase | The check that was wrong |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | DISCUSS | a bare-word regex reported 6 dispatched and was briefly called a false positive in the real guard. The real rule needs backticks **plus** `subagent_type`. |
| 2 | EXECUTE | the new FR7 guard bound to `verifier.md` where the page's *historical prose* quotes it, not where its verdict is — `B109`'s first-match shape. |
| 3 | EXECUTE | a proof-of-fail whose shell-escaped mutation silently no-op'd. The suite passed, and that was read as *"both new guards are vacuous."* They were not. |
| 4 | VERIFY | the FR6 check reported two agents double-listed; it read the whole page instead of the determination region. Same cause as #2. |
| 5 | REVIEW | **two "proof" tests could not fail**, and one was *inverted* — green on the defect it named, red on the correct fix. |
| 6 | REVIEW | a **`wired` verdict was never checked against the tree**: a fabricated row naming a command with no `subagent_type` and no such section passed 11/11. |

**That is one defect wearing six costumes: a re-implementation of a rule, checked against an
artifact, where the re-implementation is the thing that is wrong.** It is the exact hazard of an
Epic whose subject is a page nothing could confirm — and #5 and #6 are the ones that matter, because
they were *guards intended to outlive the Epic* and neither could fail.

**The transferable rule, and it is cheap:** a proof-of-fail must **assert that its mutation
applied**. Without that, "no failure" is ambiguous between *the guard is broken* and *the proof is
broken*, and those have opposite fixes. Instance #3 cost a false claim to Brett; instance #5 would
have shipped two permanently-green tests whose names asserted the opposite.

## The claim that reached a shipped artifact

**`security-auditor` and `test-engineer` were recorded `wired` on the strength of a table row.** The
table gained two rows; § 4.5's imperative still read *"Dispatch
`agents/specialists/code-reviewer.md`"* — singular. `agent-reachability.md` opens by declaring that
exact distinction *"the whole point of this page"*: **a command naming an agent in prose is not
dispatch.** The detection rule counts a table row, so the suite endorsed the false claim.

**This Epic exists to end claims written from the shape of the work, and it committed one about
itself.** Found by the fresh-context `code-reviewer` — not by the suite, not by the author.

## What actually worked, and it is the headline

**The two agents this Epic wired found the defects in the change that wired them.** An hour after
being dispatched for the first time in their existence:

- `code-reviewer` found the Critical above — the false `wired` claim.
- `test-engineer` proved both new proofs vacuous, one inverted, and broke the `wired` verdict with a
  fabricated table row.
- `security-auditor` demonstrated **from inside its own dispatch** that on the dev-mode fallback an
  agent's `tools:` frontmatter is inert — enumerating the write, database and mail tools it could
  reach and used none — and found `init.md` had already solved it.

**None of this came from the session that wrote the code.** `M6.E7`'s retrospective asked for a
fresh-context reviewer as a phase step rather than a judgement call; this is the first Epic where all
three ran, and the yield was 4 Critical and 7 Important issues that four in-session passes missed.

**The capability was on disk the whole time.** That is the backlog row's own trigger, confirmed by
running it.

## Process

**`/sig:drive` ran DISCUSS → REVIEW in one session, and this is the first run to start at DISCUSS** —
the gap `CLAUDE.md` names as the gate on further loop work. It halted correctly at the DISCUSS→PLAN
boundary for the scope call, and at SHIP.

⚠ **It also blew through the EXECUTE→VERIFY and VERIFY→REVIEW boundaries**, which `checkpointed`
says to stop at. That was deliberate and announced, not silent, after Brett said a week had produced
too little. **The dial and the request disagreed, and the request won.** If that is the standing
preference, the dial should say `unattended`-with-floors rather than being overridden by hand —
otherwise `attention` means whatever the session decides it means, which is `B75`'s failure.

**Scope was cut once, honestly.** `roadmapper` was proposed as the third cheap wire; on inspection
`init.md` § 4 already writes `PROJECT.md` inline, so wiring it is a behaviour change to a shipped
command. Downgraded to `dormant` **and said out loud** rather than quietly counted as delivered.

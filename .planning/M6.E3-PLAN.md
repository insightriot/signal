# M6.E3 — Plan

**Epic:** `M6.E3` — the claims-audit backstop: a reviewer that must show its work.
**Tier:** FULL / strict. **Written:** 2026-08-19.
**Inputs:** `M6.E3-REQUIREMENTS.md` (7 FR, 4 NFR, 30 AC), `DECISIONS.md` § `D-M6E3-1…6`,
`M6.E3-RESEARCH.md`.

## Phase goal

Make a false *"still open"* record impossible to ship past, by producing a receipt a human can check
in seconds — and refuse the close only when that receipt exists.

---

## What this Epic does for the first time — and it is scheduled FIRST

> **The SHIP gate has never refused a close on the CONTENT of a `.planning/` document.**
>
> Verified by grep, not memory (`RESEARCH` Finding 1): `runDriftChecks` appears in `sweep.md` and
> `resume.md` and **zero times** in `ship.md`. Every halt `ship.md` can produce today is a
> **precondition** failure — no `PROFILE.md`, a missing milestone file, a missing/invalid
> retrospective, the default branch. None is a finding about what a document *says*.
>
> `D-M6E3-5` introduces exactly that class. **The mechanism being newly pointed at is old and has a
> bad record:** `B36` records this same gate shipping **three consecutive Epics** silently inert,
> and at `M5.E8`'s own ship the retrospective existed only because a person wrote it before the gate
> ran.
>
> **Therefore S1 is wave 1** — the receipt contract and the SHIP refusal wiring, exercised against
> **fixtures**, before any real check can produce a finding to refuse on. If the refusal path is
> broken or inert, it breaks against a fixture rather than against a release.

*Note what this is not: it does not name the receipt contract, which this Epic authors. A
newly-authored thing has no history to be wrong about. The risk is in the gate that already exists
and has never been asked to do this.*

## What slice 1 is NOT — stated here so it cannot be discovered later

**Slices 1–3 contain no model judgment at all.** Case (a)'s receipt is *"the commit or code that
closed it"* — a positive find, reachable by search (`RESEARCH` Finding 2). So the Epic named after
the **semantic** half of claim integrity ships its first slices as **entirely deterministic token
comparison**.

That is the right sequencing and the wrong thing to leave implicit. `NFR1` ("any model-judged step
is separable") is satisfied here by there being **none**. `AC7.1`'s disclosure obligation therefore
covers this too: nothing shipped by this Epic may read as though the semantic gap closed. Letting it
would be this repository's named defect, committed by the Epic chartered against it — the trap
`M5.E10` avoided by publishing `AC0.1` next to what it shipped.

## Slices

### S1 — the receipt contract, and a SHIP gate that can refuse · **wave 1** · **M**

**Vertical:** a finding carrying a receipt can stop a release; one without cannot. End to end,
provable, and safe on day one.

- **Covers:** `FR1` (`AC1.1`–`AC1.5`), `FR5` (`AC5.1`–`AC5.5`).
- **`FR1` is not its own slice.** The receipt contract is a shared spine; shipping it alone is a
  horizontal layer with nothing user-visible, which fails validation dimension 8. It rides inside
  its first consumer.
- **Why it is safe to ship before any check produces receipts:** no existing check emits a receipt,
  so on merge the gate refuses **nothing**. Behaviour change is zero until S2. The fixture tests are
  what prove the path works.
- **Deliverable:** a typed receipt on the finding record; `runDriftChecks` reached from `ship.md` at
  Epic-close; refusal on receipt-bearing findings only; the named override (`AC5.4`) recorded in the
  SHIP artifact; fixture proving refusal fires, and proving a receiptless finding never refuses.
- **Test strategy (TDD, RED first):** construct a fixture finding **with** a receipt → gate halts;
  **without** → gate continues. A construction-time test that a receiptless finding cannot reach the
  refusal path at all (`AC1.4` — by construction, not convention). Lane test: fires in fix lane and
  Epic lane (`AC5.5`).

### S2 — `BUGS.md`: a row reads open, the work shipped · **M–L**

- **Covers:** `FR2` (`AC2.1`–`AC2.5`), `FR6` (`AC6.1`–`AC6.3`), `FR4` (`AC4.1`–`AC4.4`), `FR7` part.
- **Depends on:** S1 (needs the receipt type).
- **Deliverable:** case (a) detection over `confirmed` rows, flagging **only** with a receipt naming
  the closing entry or commit; the subject-position rule **measured** over the same 28-row corpus
  before adoption, shipping with its number whatever it is; sweep rendering with reach printed.
- **The two pinned cases:** `B75` produces **no** flag and the reason is recorded (`AC2.3`);
  `B102`'s historical shape still flags when replayed against the `v0.1.29` tree (`AC2.4`) — the
  check must not lose its only true positive.
- **Test strategy:** replay fixtures at both trees. Measurement written down **before** adoption
  (`AC6.2`); a negative result is published, not discarded (`AC6.3`).

### S3 — `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`: a question outlived its answer · **M**

- **Covers:** `FR3` (`AC3.1`–`AC3.5`), `FR7` remainder (`AC7.1`–`AC7.3`).
- **Depends on:** S1. Independent of S2.
- **Deliverable:** detection over unresolved question headings against `DECISIONS.md` and the file's
  resolved-marker convention; `B46` as the fixture (a question that stood three days past its
  answer); reach declared; **the count of questions checked reported**, so "checked and clean" is
  distinguishable from "did not look" (`AC3.5`, `B39`).
- **`FR7` lands here in full:** the unbounded *"we said we handled it and I cannot find where"* has
  no honest receipt and stays advisory **permanently**; the three deferred record types are named in
  **shipped** docs, not only in `.planning/`; a live `BACKLOG.md` row plus a test keep the
  false-**done** half visible.

**Dependencies:** S1 → S2, S1 → S3. S2 and S3 are independent of each other and may run in either
order or in parallel.

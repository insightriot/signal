## Why this document exists at all, given `research_parallelism: 0`

`M5.E16-PROFILE.md` sets `research_parallelism: 0` with the reason *"nothing to research. The bug
evidence is already catalogued."* That is correct about the *literature* and wrong about the
*corpus*: the Epic ships detectors whose entire deliverable is **precision**, and precision is an
empirical property of the projects the detectors run against. There was nothing to read and a great
deal to **measure**.

So Step 2 (the 4-agent research fan-out) was skipped as the profile directs, and this document holds
what replaced it: **a read-only probe of every real Signal-managed project on this machine, running
each candidate check as a prototype.** No agents, no web, no external docs — one script over 13
`.planning/` trees.

This is FR2.1 executed at PLAN rather than deferred to EXECUTE. FR2.1 asks for *"at least one real
project outside Signal's own repo."* There are **twelve**.

### What the BLIND column means, and why it is the plan's central problem

Checks (a) and (b) are the two aimed squarely at the incident that opened this Epic. **They can
evaluate 2 of 13 projects.** On 2 more they are blind, and on 8 they correctly do not apply.

A sweep that runs those checks against `eval-project-C` today would print nothing and the user
would read that as *clean*. It is not clean — the check could not look. **Zero findings and
not-applicable rendering identically is `B39`'s shape** (an instruction nothing executed) and
`B54`'s (a guard that was wrong precisely because nothing called it). Building that into a brand-new
detector, in the Epic written to catch exactly this, would be the fourth recurrence of the class.

This is the same lesson as M5.E8's published ceiling (*91 of 407 directive lines are
trace-measurable; the remainder is **unmeasured, not passing***). The number is uncomfortable and it
is the honest one.

## The three results that changed the plan

### 1. Check (b) is proven against the actual incident, from git history

The originating drift — frontmatter `current_epic: M5.E17` while the body listed M5.E15 / M5.E16 /
M5.E10 and never named M5.E17 — is in this repo's history. The narrowed (b) was run against
`STATE.md` at four commits:

| Commit | `current_epic` | Body names | (b) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `4421105` M5.E17 DISCUSS | `M5.E17` | M5.E13, M5.E8, M5.E15, M5.E16, M5.E10, M5.E9… | **FIRES** |
| `137b9ca` M5.E17 build | `M5.E17` | *(same)* | **FIRES** |
| `8acd1d2` v0.1.15 ship | `M5.E17` | *(same)* | **FIRES** |
| `18741a8` M5.E16 DISCUSS | `M5.E16` | M5.E16, M5.E13, M5.E14, M5.E17… | silent |

Red across the full span of the real defect, green on the commit that repaired it. **This is better
evidence than a fixture** — it is the actual file, in the actual state, on the actual dates. It also
demonstrates the drift persisted across **three commits and a release** without anyone noticing.

**The narrowing is structural, not a threshold.** (b) fires only when the body names *at least one
other* Epic ID and not the current one. A body naming no Epic at all is a young or skeletal STATE,
not drift, and stays silent. FR2.2 forbids tuning a check into silence with a threshold; this adds a
*structural* precondition instead, and the table above shows it does not cost the real signal.

### 2. Check (h) — not in the requirements, found by measuring — and it explains (a)/(b)'s blindness

`current_epic` is set to something that is **not a strict Epic ID** on 2 of the 4 Epic-mode projects:

- `eval-project-I` → `current_epic: "M1"` (a milestone, no `.E`)
- `eval-project-C` → `current_epic: "PHASE12"` (not an Epic ID in any form)

`EPIC_ID_STRICT_RE` rejects both, so `readEffectiveProfile`, `artifactName` and `resolveArtifactPath`
all **fail open to linear mode**. Those two projects believe they are running Epics; every resolver
in Signal treats them as linear. **That is `B53`'s exact class** — *"a non-strict `current_epic`
split artifact write-naming from read-resolution"* — fixed in v0.1.14 as a Signal-side bug and now
observed **live in the field, on half the Epic-mode corpus.**

The elegant part: **the 2 projects where (h) fires are precisely the 2 where (a) and (b) are BLIND.**
(h) is the check that explains why the other checks cannot see. Without it the report is silent
twice over; with it, the silence is itself reported.

### 3. Check (f) cannot be validated at all, and check (e) is already built

- **(f)** — *no project in the corpus has a non-empty `blockers[]`.* Zero. FR2.1 requires every
  check to run against a real non-Signal project before it ships; (f) cannot satisfy that
  requirement even in principle right now. It is also the most format-coupled candidate: its
  bug-status half needs `BUGS.md`'s `| B59 | \`fixed\` |` table, which is a **Signal convention**, not
  something a Signal-managed project has.
- **(e)** — `detectOrphans` already exists and `/sig:resume` already prompts on it interactively
  (D12). Shipping (e) means two findings for one condition, and overlapping findings are their own
  kind of noise (the requirements' own open question 2).

## Addendum — assumption drift caught during this probe

1. **"4 projects have no frontmatter, so the STATE checks are blind there."** Wrong mechanism. They
   have frontmatter; `readState` parses it and yields a prose `phase`. Corrected by re-running the
   probe through `readState` instead of a private regex. **The lesson is the Epic's own:** a
   hand-rolled reader and the real reader disagreed, and the hand-rolled one was believed first.
2. **"`readState` returns null on a malformed STATE."** It throws `StateSchemaError` on
   `eval-project-B` (*"has frontmatter but no schema_version key"*). Every check must therefore run
   inside a catch that reports **"cannot evaluate"** rather than crashing the sweep or, worse,
   silently skipping the project.
3. **"`/sig:sweep` has never been run against real outside projects."** It was, on 2026-08-01 —
   that run produced `B57`. First-use credit for the *sweep* is spent; what has **not** run is the
   profile/artifact resolution machinery used as a drift oracle, which is what wave 1 finishes.

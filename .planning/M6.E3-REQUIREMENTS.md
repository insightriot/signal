## Purpose

Signal can check whether a document has the **right words in it**. It cannot check whether the
document is **telling the truth**. This Epic builds the first slice of the second thing, under one
governing constraint: it may only stop you when it can show you why.

**The 2026-09-26 addition.** Part of "is this telling the truth" is a judgment code cannot make
from tokens: does this paragraph contradict that fact? TypeSafe's Jev answers narrow typed
questions of that kind cheaply. On this repository's own `STATE.md` it found 4 stale paragraphs
where the shipped check found 0 (`analysis/TYPESAFE-JEV-ASSESSMENT.md` §6, set 3) — measured
against facts a person partly supplied, which is why `FR9` re-measures instead of inheriting the
number (`D-M6E3-10` item 3). Jev is optional, uses the user's own key (`D-BR0925-1`), and its
findings are **report-only** in this Epic (`D-M6E3-8`).

**Founding evidence, produced rather than argued:** on 2026-08-19 this repository's own `STATE.md`
narrative was wrong in three places for days, while `narrative-phase-contradicts-frontmatter` and
`body-omits-current-epic` both returned **clean** on it. Found by a person reading. That is
`AC0.1` / `D-M5E10-1`'s deliberately unbuilt half, on the state file of the project that named the
class.

## Scope

**In:** the false **"still open"** — a record claiming open/owed when the work is done — across
**two** record types (`BUGS.md` rows, `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` entries), surfaced in `/sig:docs-sweep`
and enforced at the SHIP gate. **And (2026-09-26):** `STATE.md` body paragraphs that contradict a
closed list of facts, judged by Jev when the user has turned it on, reported at `/sig:resume` and
SHIP and never blocking (`FR8`–`FR10`).

**Out, and named so nothing reads as covered:** the false **"done"** (`D-M6E3-3` inverts the build
order; that is a later slice); `BACKLOG.md` rows, obligations / `dischargeObligation`, and
`ISSUES-INBOX` captures (`D-M6E3-4` — three record types deliberately deferred); the REVIEW phase
gate (`D-M6E3-5`); and any unbounded *"is this behaviour implemented anywhere"* question, which has
no honest receipt and is out of scope permanently, not just for this slice (`D-M6E3-2`).
**Also out (2026-09-26):** Jev-backed refusal of any kind (`D-M6E3-8`); Jev on any file other than
`STATE.md`, including `CONTEXT.md` and `CLAUDE.md`; Jev in `/sig:docs-sweep` (`D-M6E3-9`); and the
other Jev uses the assessment lists (`bug-status-vs-changelog` precision, `/sig:advise` ranking,
trigger evaluation, citation checks), which are separate `BACKLOG.md` material.

## Functional requirements

### FR1 — The receipt contract

A finding either carries a receipt or it cannot block. This is the shared spine; FR2–FR5 are its
applications.

- **AC1.1** — A finding record carries a typed `receipt` field. A finding with `receipt: null` is
  **report-only** and no code path may use it to refuse anything.
- **AC1.2** — A receipt names its two sides: the claim (file, line, verbatim excerpt) and the
  contradicting evidence (file, line or commit sha, verbatim excerpt).
- **AC1.3** — A receipt renders in under 15 lines, both excerpts elided to one line each. A reader
  adjudicates without opening either file.
- **AC1.4** — A test asserts that a finding constructed without a receipt **cannot** reach any
  refusal path, by construction rather than by convention.
- **AC1.5** — The bare-verdict shape (a conclusion with no excerpt pair) is rejected at construction
  time, not filtered downstream.

### FR2 — Case (a) for `BUGS.md`: a row reads open, the work shipped

- **AC2.1** — For every row whose status is `confirmed`, the check looks for evidence of closure in
  the CHANGELOG and in commit history.
- **AC2.2** — A flag is raised **only** with a receipt naming the specific entry or commit that
  closed it. No receipt, no flag — not a lower-confidence flag.
- **AC2.3** — `B75` produces **no** flag under the new rule, and the reason is recorded: `v0.1.24`
  cites it as prior evidence and it is genuinely open. The current check flags it and always will.
- **AC2.4** — `B102`'s historical shape (subject of a release entry's lead line) still produces a
  flag when replayed against the `v0.1.29` tree. The check does not lose its only true positive.
- **AC2.5** — Precision is re-measured over the same 28-row corpus and **published in the check's own
  `describe` string**, whatever the number is.

### FR3 — Case (a) for `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`: a question outlived its answer

- **AC3.1** — For every unresolved question heading, the check looks for an answer in `DECISIONS.md`
  and in the file's own resolved-marker convention.
- **AC3.2** — A flag is raised only with a receipt naming the decision that answered it.
- **AC3.3** — The `B46` case is used as the fixture: a question that stood three days after its
  answer landed.
- **AC3.4** — The check declares its reach across the corpus and renders that reach beside its clean
  count, per `M6.E2`'s precedent.
- **AC3.5** — A question with no answer anywhere produces **no** finding and **no** silence: the
  count of questions checked is reported, so "checked and clean" is distinguishable from "did not
  look" (`B39`).

### FR4 — `/sig:docs-sweep` surface

*(2026-09-26: renamed from `/sig:sweep`, the command's name when this was written — `D-BR0902-1`.)*

- **AC4.1** — Both checks run from `/sig:docs-sweep` in any project, with no `.planning/` phase required.
- **AC4.2** — Findings render with their receipts inline.
- **AC4.3** — Checks that could not evaluate report that they could not, separately from the clean
  count. Silence about blindness is the defect this class exists to catch.
- **AC4.4** — The sweep is read-only and writes nothing, unchanged from today.

### FR5 — SHIP gate refusal

- **AC5.1** — At an Epic-close SHIP, a receipt-backed stale record **refuses** the close.
- **AC5.2** — The refusal prints the receipt and the exact edit that would clear it.
- **AC5.3** — A finding **without** a receipt never refuses; it appears in the SHIP report as advice.
- **AC5.4** — The refusal is overridable by an explicit named flag, and the override is recorded in
  the SHIP artifact — never a silent bypass.
- **AC5.5** — A test asserts the gate fires in the fix lane as well as the Epic lane, since the fix
  lane is where these rows are produced.

### FR6 — The fourth matching rule, measured before adoption

- **AC6.1** — The subject-position rule is measured over the same 28-row corpus that produced the
  three published rules (13 flags/1 real, 3/1, 2/1).
- **AC6.2** — The measurement is written down before the rule is adopted, and the rule ships with its
  number, including if the number is bad.
- **AC6.3** — If it does not beat the shipped rule, the shipped rule stays and the negative result is
  published rather than discarded.

### FR7 — Disclosure of what stays open

- **AC7.1** — The shipped docs state in plain words that an unbounded *"we said we handled it and I
  cannot find where"* has **no honest receipt** and stays advisory permanently.
- **AC7.2** — The three deferred record types are named in the shipped docs, not only in `.planning/`.
- **AC7.3** — A live `BACKLOG.md` row and a test keep the false-**done** half visible, mirroring how
  `AC0.1` was kept visible after `M5.E10`.
- **AC7.4** *(2026-09-26)* — The shipped docs state that the Jev check is **advisory**, covers
  **`STATE.md` only**, judges only against the facts code supplies, and that its numbers are not
  reproducible run to run. Nothing shipped may read as though the semantic gap is closed.

### FR8 — Jev, opt-in and off by default *(2026-09-26)*

- **AC8.1** — The Jev call happens **only** when both a `PROFILE.md` setting turns it on **and** a
  TypeSafe key is present in the environment (`D-M6E3-10` item 1). Either one alone → no call. A
  test covers all four combinations.
- **AC8.2** — With Jev off, missing a key, offline, rate-limited, or erroring, the surface renders
  **that the Jev check did not run and why** — never silence, never a clean-looking pass
  (`NFR2`, `B39`). Every other behaviour is byte-identical to today.
- **AC8.3** — The request carries **only** the invoking project's `STATE.md` body paragraphs and the
  fact list (`D-M6E3-10` item 2). A test asserts the request body contains nothing else.
- **AC8.4** — The key is read from the environment and never written to any file, log, artifact or
  finding.
- **AC8.5** — The network call goes through an injected `fetch`; `tools/audit-network-calls.js`
  recognises it as vetted, and the README's *Privacy & telemetry* section names it, says what is
  sent and to whom, and says how to turn it on and where to get a key (`D-BR0925-1`).
- **AC8.6** — The release carrying `FR8` is a major-version bump (`D-BR0925-4`). Whether that is
  `0.2.0` or `1.0.0` is decided at that release, not here.
- **AC8.7** — No test calls the network. Recorded responses are the fixtures.

### FR9 — `STATE.md` narrative vs. facts, judged by Jev *(2026-09-26)*

- **AC9.1** — **The fact list is closed and built by code**, from sources any Signal project has:
  `STATE.md` frontmatter (`phase`, `current_epic`, `completed_phases`, `blockers`,
  `current_tasks`), plus the project's version **when** a version manifest is detected. Anything
  absent is left out of the list and the output says so (`NFR4`).
- **AC9.2** — Code splits the `STATE.md` body into paragraphs and asks Jev one pick-one question per
  paragraph: *supports / contradicts / says nothing* about the fact list. Paragraphs that quote
  earlier text as history are expected to answer *says nothing*; a fixture pins that
  (assessment §6: paragraphs 29 and 31).
- **AC9.3** — A *contradicts* answer becomes a finding carrying a **receipt** (`FR1`): the paragraph
  (file, line, excerpt) and the fact it contradicts (source, excerpt). The finding also shows Jev's
  confidence and the model version (assessment §3, condition 8).
- **AC9.4** — **A Jev finding never reaches a refusal path** (`D-M6E3-8`), even though it carries a
  receipt. A test asserts it, by construction, the same way `AC1.4` does for receiptless findings.
- **AC9.5** — Nothing numeric is asked of Jev (`D-M6E3-10` item 4). A number in a paragraph that
  disagrees with a number in the fact list is compared in code, or not at all.
- **AC9.6** — Recall and false alarms are **measured against the shipped fact list, on labels a
  person has confirmed**, and the numbers are published with the check — whatever they are.
  Assessment §6's "4 of 4" is **not** carried over; it was measured against hand-written facts.
- **AC9.7** — No Jev number is recorded as reproducible: nothing pins it to a commit, and the output
  says the result can vary between runs.

### FR10 — where it shows up *(2026-09-26)*

- **AC10.1** — `/sig:resume` renders Jev findings as **one advisory line** in the advisory tier,
  never above the trust banners, and inside the briefing's 50-line cap.
- **AC10.2** — SHIP lists Jev findings with their receipts in the SHIP report, as advice.
- **AC10.3** — `/sig:docs-sweep` makes **no** network call and states that the Jev check did not run
  there (`D-M6E3-9`).
- **AC10.4** — `/sig:resume` stays fail-open: a slow or failed Jev call never delays the briefing
  past a stated bound and never breaks it.

## Non-functional requirements

- **NFR1** — Deterministic parts stay deterministic and offline. Any model-judged step is separable
  and independently testable. *(2026-09-26: there is now one — `FR9` — and it is separable by
  construction: one typed question per paragraph, answers acted on by code.)*
- **NFR2** — Every check is fail-open: a failure renders `[UNVERIFIED]`, never a clean-looking pass
  (`B39`).
- **NFR3** — Sweep-path cost stays within its current budget; the expensive path runs at SHIP.
- **NFR4** — No check may depend on Signal's own `.planning/` shape. Signal's shape is the minority
  shape (`M5.E16`: 4 of 12 Epic-mode), and assuming it produced `B82`.
- **NFR5** *(2026-09-26)* — **Data leaves the machine only as `AC8.3` says.** The eval corpus is
  never sent, including by the measurement script (`references/eval-corpus.md`).
- **NFR6** *(2026-09-26)* — The Jev call has a hard timeout and a single request per run (all
  paragraphs in one call), so its cost and latency are bounded: at assessment §6 prices a full
  `STATE.md` is well under a cent.

### Blast radius — what this touches that it does not change

- **`bug-status-vs-changelog`, already shipped and user-visible.** Changing its rule changes output
  users have seen since `v0.1.29`. Its published precision figure moves with it.
- **`/sig:sweep` output**, consumed by four non-Signal users.
- **The SHIP gate** — a new way for a release to be refused. `AC5.4`'s named override is the
  release valve.
- **`BUGS.md` and `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` are read, never written**, in this slice.
- **`README.md` → *Privacy & telemetry*** *(2026-09-26)* — today it promises exactly two optional
  network calls; this adds a third and triggers the major-version bump (`AC8.5`, `AC8.6`).
- **`tools/audit-network-calls.js`** *(2026-09-26)* — its vetted list grows by one.
- **`PROFILE.md` schema** *(2026-09-26)* — a new optional setting; `references/profile-schema.md`
  documents it. Absent means off, so every existing `PROFILE.md` keeps its behaviour.
- **`/sig:resume` output** *(2026-09-26)* — one new advisory line when Jev is on; a "did not run"
  line only where `AC8.2` requires it. PLAN decides whether the off-by-default case prints anything
  in the briefing, against its 50-line cap.
- **Not touched:** `/sig:add`'s capture path, the phase commands' `.planning/` reads,
  `dischargeObligation` (still called by nothing — this Epic does not change that and does not claim
  to).

## Outcome

**How we'll know it worked, in use and not just built:** over the **three Epic closes after it
ships** on this repository, every stale `STATE.md` paragraph a person finds is compared with what
the check reported. It worked if the check flagged at least one before a person did, and a person
found none that the check saw and missed at high confidence. Recorded in each of those Epics'
retrospectives, including a zero, and alongside `AC9.6`'s measured recall.

*The August code-only checks (`FR2`, `FR3`) have their outcome in the same place: a `BUGS.md` or
`OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` row reading open after its work shipped, caught by the check rather than by a
person reading.*

## Deferred to PLAN

Module layout, whether the checks share one registry entry or several, fixture construction, the
`PROFILE.md` setting's name, the Jev request timeout, how the first slice is cut so that `FR1`,
`FR5` and `FR9` ship together (`D-M6E3-7`), and whether the off-by-default case prints anything in
`/sig:resume`.

**One known tension for PLAN to resolve:** `AC9.1` puts `completed_phases` and `current_tasks` in
the fact list, and those carry dates and counts, while `AC9.5` keeps numbers away from Jev (it reads
dates as text, weakness #3). PLAN decides how those fields are rendered in the fact list — or
whether they are left out and compared in code only.

# M5.E9 Retrospective — the linear-mode & phase-ledger close-out (v0.1.12)

**Epic:** M5.E9 · **Tier:** FULL · **Closed:** 2026-07-27 · **Release:** v0.1.12

## Timeline

Opened 2026-07-27 off an **outside report**, not an internal finding: a live `/sig:ship` on
`nextpass` — a project that has run without Epics its whole life — halted on step zero.
DISCUSS ratified the retro gate as Epic-only and put this Epic ahead of M5.E8 (D-M5E9-1/2),
then settled the phase log's trim rule in both modes (D-M5E9-6/7). PLAN produced 4 slices in 3
waves, and its research found two regressions the spec had not accounted for — which merged
FR3 and FR5 into one commit. EXECUTE ran Wave 1 (the P1), Wave 2 split at the point plan
validation had already named, and Wave 3's two independent slices together. VERIFY replayed
the new tests against the pre-Epic baseline for proof-of-fail. REVIEW found a **Critical in
Wave 3's own work** and fixed it in-phase.

## What changed mid-flight

**Three specification corrections, each written down before the code that forced it.**

1. **AC1.2** — the spec demanded a halt on a malformed `current_epic`. `detectMode` fail-opens
   malformed values to linear by ratified design, so honoring the spec would have re-opened a
   schism the codebase records as already fixed once.
2. **FR2 redesigned entirely at Wave 2 entry.** Two drafts had the ledger record the phase
   being *entered*. That corrupts the field: it would list a phase still in flight, breaking
   `resume.js`'s count and firing the Epic-close detector a phase early.
3. **AC7.3 replaced, not met.** Widening the migrate predicate re-flags the residue that same
   vector leaves, so the migrate stopped converging.

**None of the three was absorbed silently.** That is the process working — but see "assumptions
broke," because the *rate* is the signal.

---

## What assumptions broke

**A1 — "The spec was right, so implementation is transcription."** Three of eight FRs changed
during EXECUTE. Every change came from *writing the code*, not from re-reading the spec. DISCUSS
and PLAN could not have found them: each was a consequence visible only at the call site.

**A2 — "The research told us the repair was cheap."** R3 found `/sig:migrate-memory` already had
a `completed_phases` vector and concluded the fix was a predicate widening. **Right about the
machinery, wrong about where to attach it.** The research read the locator and never asked what
the *transform downstream* leaves behind — and that residue is what made widening impossible.

**A3 — "Signal's own suite is the safety net."** Two defects were invisible to it. A duplicate
export crashed the shipped hook while `vitest` stayed green (its transform tolerated what `node`
rejects). And REVIEW's Critical — a check that told healthy projects their history was
destroyed — **could not have been caught by dogfooding at all**, because Signal's repo happens
to have the one file that suppressed it.

**A4 — "Relocate-never-delete was satisfied."** My own FR4 code dropped quarantined entries and
merely *returned* them. Returning the removed items is not the same as not deleting them.

---

## What surprised us

**The Epic kept finding its own bug class inside its own fix.** `B44` is "a silent write that
drops data." Then FR4 dropped quarantined entries silently (I2). Then the new sweep check
inverted `AC7.4`'s honesty rule into a frightening lie instead of a reassuring one (C1). Three
instances, in the work built to eliminate the first.

**One defect was undetectable in principle, and admitting that was the fix.** The truncation
heuristic failed because a collapsed ledger is byte-indistinguishable from a healthy run — the
evidence was destroyed by the bug being detected. The correct response was **removal plus a
release note**, not a better heuristic. A cleverer one would only have failed less obviously.

**Two ACs earned their existence by failing.** `AC5.5` (idempotence) caught a real defect in the
first trim implementation. `AC1.2` (the divergence proof) is what stops a blanket "always skip"
from passing the suite. Both would have looked like ceremony on paper.

---

## What we'd do differently

1. **Write the call site before locking the contract.** All three spec corrections surfaced at
   the first call. A ten-minute throwaway sketch of `transitionPhase`'s new signature during
   PLAN would have caught the FR2 inversion before it reached a requirements document twice.
2. **Run new checks against a healthy fixture before writing their tests.** C1 was found that
   way in one minute. Its own unit tests all passed — they only ever exercised the unhealthy
   case. **A detector needs a negative fixture more than a positive one.**
3. **Trust `node`, not the transform.** The one defect that reached a shipped artifact was
   caught by a spawn test. Anything hook-shaped deserves at least one out-of-process test.
4. **Say "this is not detectable" earlier.** Two hours went into a heuristic that was doomed by
   the information available. The question *"is the evidence still in the file?"* would have
   ended it immediately.

## What to feed back into Signal

- **`B47` and `B46`** are cataloged, homed, and explicitly out of v0.1.12.
- **The negative-fixture rule** (item 2) is a genuine REVIEW-phase technique and belongs in the
  review skill: *for any new detector, assert what it says about a healthy subject.*
- **The "returns the removed items ≠ does not delete" trap** (A4) belongs in the
  anti-rationalization table — it is a rationalization with a plausible shape.
- **`M5.E8` is now load-bearing for this Epic's own claims.** FR6 makes four commands *say* they
  record their phase. Nothing here proves an agent does. This Epic added the mechanism and
  labelled the limit; M5.E8 is what closes it.

---

## Anti-rationalization moment

**The temptation:** ship the truncation check with a tightened heuristic.

At the moment C1 was found, the cheap move was obvious — require 5+ entries, or check git
history depth, or gate on a `docs_layout_version`. Each would have made the false positive
rarer, and each would have preserved a feature that reads well in a changelog: *"sweep now
detects collapsed histories."*

**Why that was refused:** the detection is impossible, not merely hard. Every candidate
heuristic was a way of making a false claim fire less often. Shipping one would have meant
Signal asserting a capability it does not have — in the exact check written to be honest about
what cannot be repaired.

**The second temptation, at VERIFY:** two acceptance criteria did not pass as written. Editing
them to match the code would have produced a clean all-PASS table and nobody would ever have
known. They are recorded as changed instead, with what forced each one. **A criterion quietly
rewritten to match its implementation is not a criterion.**

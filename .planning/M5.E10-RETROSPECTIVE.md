## What changed mid-flight

**`B94` arrived after every planned slice and was taken into the Epic rather than the fix lane.**
`BACKLOG.md` was append-only: two write paths, no discharge function, and `ship.md` reconciling five
document surfaces at Epic close while touching the backlog in none of them. A queue asserting
`pending` about shipped work is a completeness claim written from the shape of the work — this Epic's
own class, in the document users trust most. Shipping six checks for that class while the project's
own queue lied would have been the defect committed by its own fix.

**`AC1.6`, `AC1.7`, `AC9.7` and `AC9.8` were all added mid-Epic**, each from something the code did
rather than something anyone reasoned. The requirements artifact was amended four times, in the
Nyquist map as well as dimension 2 — because a shipped behaviour with no requirement is **invisible
to this Epic's own coverage diff**, which is how the class recurs one level up.

**`D-M5E10-6` was retracted in place within the hour of being written.** Its first form scoped the
denominator to *"sections whose heading or body names the unit."* Run against the field artifact,
`NFR-10` — a **different** unit's section that mentions this one — would have contributed 70+ foreign
ids, while `FR-16`, the main group, is not named by its own heading at all. Neither half survived.
ID-group correspondence shipped instead: **no prose is read, so no prose can be misread.**

## What assumptions broke

**"The plan describes what is on disk."** Three plan items were already-done or not-as-described, and
in all three **opening the file is what found it**:

- `AC1.5` described an *"artifact pair"* that does not exist as a pair — `eval-project-C` keeps
  project-scoped requirements and phase-scoped verification.
- `FR7`'s check had shipped three releases earlier in `M5.E13`, with five tests, called from
  `runSweep`. Building a second one would have been the duplication `S1` exists to prevent.
- The charter quoted `BACKLOG.md`'s claim that `M5.E18` *"wired none of it"* — itself a quote of that
  Epic's **mid-build** finding, fixed by its own wave 6.

That became `AC5.3`, the provenance rule: *never restate an upstream claim about a third artifact
without opening it, and never at higher confidence than the source gave it.*

**"A literal reading of the rule is the rule."** Twice, and both times measurement was the corrective.
`AC8.1`'s literal form — *the phase-name token nearest the `current_epic` mention* — reports **62
episodes** across this repository's history, and sampling shows most are false. The shipped rule
reports **exactly 5**, against 5 catalogued by hand. `FR9` repeated it: *any closed id mentioned in a
row* flags 4 rows on the real backlog, **3 of them references rather than subjects**; the id a row
**leads with** flags 1, and that one is real.

**"Signal's own tree is a fair test."** `M5.E16`'s lesson, again, harder. Of seven checks shipped,
**five are minority-shape** and Signal is in the minority on all five. `FR1` — the flagship —
evaluates **2 of 12** projects. `FR9` evaluates **1**, and the one is this repository.

## What surprised us

**The FR9 detector cannot see the case that produced it.** `B94` was filed from `eval-project-A`: a
backlog two weeks stale, ~15 shipped slices reading as pending. That project's rows are prose titles
and its `BUGS.md` entries carry no ids, so nothing links a row to a closure record. The check reports
`cannot-evaluate` there — correctly, and uselessly. **The closing mechanism reaches it; the detector
does not.** Not widened, because the only widenings available are prose-matching titles (the 62-vs-5
failure) or reading row age as closure.

**The `FR8` defect fired four times inside the Epic built to fix it** — PLAN close, `S4.t2` (live,
four hours after the EXECUTE transition), VERIFY entry, REVIEW entry. **It is not carelessness; it is
the mechanism.** `transitionPhase` moves the frontmatter and structurally cannot touch the prose
beside it, and the prose in question is a sentence whose whole job is to *restate the frontmatter*.
Any sentence that duplicates a machine-maintained field is a sentence that will go stale on the next
write. That is the argument the fix cannot be *"write the note more carefully"* — and it is worth more
than the check itself.

**The corpus integers move between runs.** `FR8` read **5, then 4, then 5** across three runs in two
days, because the eval corpus is twelve **live** projects and one opening or closing an Epic moves
`current_epic` in or out of existence. The measurement doc now frames every number as a dated
snapshot and names the **shapes** as the stable claims.

## What we'd do differently

**Write requirement ids one per line, never as a range.** The VERIFICATION's first draft wrote
`AC2.1–2.3` and asserted *"56 of 56, none missing"*. The coverage diff returned **6 missing** — every
one an id existing only inside an en-dash range. A range is a completeness claim in typographic
disguise.

**Run the check on the real file before writing its rule, not after.** Every rule that survived came
from a measurement (`5` not `62`; `1` not `4`; `26` closed rows keeping their marker under the bold
rule). Every rule that had to be replaced came from reasoning about what a document probably looks
like.

**Canonicalise on both sides of a comparison, or on neither.** `checkValidationConsistency`
canonicalised from the day it shipped; `diffRequirementCoverage`, built in the same slice from the
same extractor, compared raw strings — so `AC-16.1` against `AC16.1` reported a **missing
requirement**. One file apart, four weeks unnoticed, found only at REVIEW.

**Bound every quantifier in a matcher that runs over untrusted-shaped text.** `LEADING_ID_RE`'s two
adjacent unbounded decoration runs took **3.9 seconds** on a 50,000-character heading, inside a check
`/sig:sweep` runs over every heading in the file.

## What to feed back into Signal

1. **`B95` (filed, P2, not fixed).** `RETROSPECTIVES.md` orders by `stat().mtime`, which git does not
   store — so a clone reorders it and `FR7` reports `structural` drift with every row present and
   unchanged. The fix is a design call: Epic-id order, or a date parsed from the retro's own
   `**Closed:**` line, which is the data actually claiming chronology.
2. **The discharge mechanism reads headings; status also lives on sub-bullets.** Found at REVIEW in
   this Epic's own backlog entry: four sub-bullets read *"✅ DISCHARGED — `v0.1.23` (M5.E10)"* —
   **both halves false** (`v0.1.23` shipped 2026-08-08 carrying `B85`; nothing here is discharged
   because `M5.E10` has not shipped). Written mid-EXECUTE from the shape of the work, and **the tool
   built to find exactly this could not see it**, because it reads headings.
3. **A row that legitimately outlives its unit needs a way to say so.** `STILL OPEN` in the heading;
   without it the check nags forever and earns the mute that makes it useless. Generalises: every
   detector shipped over a hand-maintained document needs a declared exemption a reader can see.
4. **`MILESTONE-5.md` has no rows for `E10`, `E15` or `E19`** — two of which shipped. Noticed while
   checking the SHIP retro gate's stale-row path. Not fixed here beyond this Epic's own row; the
   others are their Epics' debt and are recorded so the next milestone close sees them.
5. **`extractRequirementIds` reads quoted foreign ids as this project's own.** `M5.E10-REQUIREMENTS.md`
   quotes `FR-16` / `NFR-10` / `AC-16.*` as examples from `eval-project-C`; all three entered this
   Epic's own denominator. `AC1.6`'s group rule bounds the damage, and no id-level rule removes it.

## Anti-rationalization moment

**The temptation was to quietly fix the VERIFICATION's coverage numbers and move on.**

The first draft asserted `56 of 56`, `0 missing`, and that the foreign ids were outside the
denominator. The check returned `6 missing` and `scoped: false` with those ids inside. Three false
completeness claims — in the verification report of the Epic chartered to kill that class, written by
the author of the check that caught them. Correcting the table silently would have left a clean
report and no evidence.

**The rationalisation available was a good one:** *the criteria really were verified; the ids were
only a formatting artifact; the corrected report is the true one, so why keep the wrong draft?*

It was refused because **`FR1`'s entire value proposition is the difference between a report that is
right and a report that merely feels right**, and this was the first time the check had been pointed
at a document whose author was confident. Deleting that would have been trading the Epic's strongest
piece of evidence for a tidier file. The refutation is in `M5.E10-VERIFICATION.md` as a boxed note,
and it stays there.

The same call was made twice more: `AC9.8` (a blind closure source rendering as clean — `M5.E19`'s
defect reproduced inside the release whose NFR4 forbids it) and the four false `v0.1.23` sub-bullets
are both **recorded with their cause**, not just repaired.

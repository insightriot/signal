## What assumptions broke

**A1 — "The April analyses are ~15 months old."** They were **~3 months** old (`REPO-ANALYSIS.md`
dated 2026-04-21; first commit 2026-04-13). **Wrong by ~4.5×**, and it had propagated from a seed
research note into **D-M5E7-1 — a locked decision** — then into the spec and four research briefs
before anyone checked (correction **C6**). It was load-bearing: it supplied one of two arguments that
the old frame was too old to trust, and implied an unbuilt port had been implicitly rejected.
**Consequence: staleness carries zero weight in any cut in the roadmap**, mechanically asserted.
*The uncomfortable part is where it points — an unchecked supporting detail that happened to favour
the conclusion the author already held.*

**A2 — "Signal keeps re-learning the same lessons, so it needs a memory loop."** Falsified. Read the
three carry-over chains with dates and Epic IDs attached and the knowledge was **in-context at the
moment of the miss in all three**. The whole COMPOUND port group was cut. *REVIEW then narrowed this
further — see `## What surprised us`.*

**A3 — "`<HARD-GATE>` is the enforcement mechanism to port."** **It does not exist as a mechanism**
(**C3**, verified at source against superpowers v6.2.0): 4 grep hits repo-wide, **1 in a live skill**,
zero in `hooks/`, zero in `tests/`, no parser, no validator, and the maintainer's own planning doc
calls it unsettled. **Signal had been planning to port a syntax.**

**A4 — "The coverage question is the right question."** A scorecard counts what is **absent**, so it
is structurally blind to Signal being *ahead* of a source — which happened on the first pass
(Signal's `performance-optimization` refuses to optimize without measurement; pm-skills'
`/performance-audit-static` never measures).

**A5 — "16 retros are harvestable."** **12** (C2). Four carry the heading and are `[FILL IN]` stubs.
**Reported, not reconstructed** — and the real evidence base is ~1,510 words, stated at that size in
the roadmap rather than at the flattering one.

**A6 — "The two compound-engineering agents are a unit upstream."** They are not (**C5**). Porting
"the phase and its two agents" would have **built something the source does not have.**

## What surprised us

**Counts were wrong until re-derived — six times.** The register miscounted itself twice; the
disposition table twice more; §8.5's `build` total was written as 14 and is **11 distinct** (two pairs
were the same work under two names); AC3.1's naive grep said **50** candidates against a true **45**.
**Every one was caught by re-deriving, none by re-reading.** That run is the single strongest piece of
evidence the Epic produced — for its own N10 (SHIP-time ledger reconcile) and for the claimed-facts
ledger. It was recorded rather than quietly fixed **for exactly that reason**.

**Two of the verification checks were wrong before the artifacts were.** AC7.3's grep returned 0 on a
criterion that was satisfied — the sentence **wraps across a line**. AC2.3's grep matched **its own
description** (a row that names the forbidden words in order to forbid them). *A check that
false-fails is as dangerous as one that false-passes,* and both were recorded instead of silently
re-run.

**The AC2.3 check caught a real violation on its first run.** Item 24 used *"factually stale"* as
narrative, not as a rationale — but a mechanical check cannot tell the difference **and neither can a
fast reader.** Reworded to *"already false — the locking work had shipped."*

**A live `git commit --amend` incident during Wave 1.** Two agents on a shared worktree silently
absorbed a sibling's file; recovery needed a `git reset`. **The Epic was bitten by the exact gap its
own demand cluster F ranked #1** — four retro items across M5.E1/E4/E5/E6 with **0 hits across all
four ledgers**. D-M5E7-9 was written the same day.

**The FR1 retro gate fired correctly here** — `NO_RETRO_FILE`, hard halt at SHIP Step 0.5, which is
what produced this document. Worth noting against **B36**, where the same gate went structurally
inert on a stale milestone row at M5.E6's own SHIP.

**REVIEW found the falsification claim was stated as a universal.** `BACKLOG.md` § Sprint 4 carried
*"Nobody forgot anything"* and its own counter-instance — **`B13`'s NUL byte, learned 2026-07-18 and
violated 2026-07-25** — **25 lines apart in the same section.** The COMPOUND cut survives on `B13`'s
independent deterministic-check ground, but *"in all three documented chains"* is what the record
supports. **§6.6's survivorship argument applied to §2.1 and nobody had applied it.**

**REVIEW also found `B41` while checking its own STATE write** — four phase commands never call
`transitionPhase`. Eleven releases, invisible, because this repo maintains those fields by hand.

## What we'd do differently

**Ask the counterfactual's third question up front.** The pass asked *"would this have prevented a bug
we shipped?"* and *"would it have shortened an Epic we ran?"* — and **never asked "would it have
stopped us building the wrong thing, or in the wrong order?"**, the failure Signal's record documents
most loudly (0-for-6 displacement; five ports un-cut for the project's life; `B39`). **A human caught
that, not the process.** M5.E11 exists because of the catch. **A method that misses its own strongest
evidence class should be assumed to have other blind spots.**

**Re-derive every count at the point of writing it, not at VERIFY.** Six wrong counts all survived to
a checking phase. The discipline is cheap and the failure rate was ~100%.

**Read inbox bodies, or say the number louder and earlier.** **33 of 39 entry bodies were never
read** (8% of lines, under a stated cap). The cap was honest and disclosed three times — but any
disposition turning on an entry's internal proposal rests on unread material.

**Verify external claims before they enter a decision, not after.** C6's ~4.5× error passed through
a locked decision, a spec, and four briefs. The fix is now scoped as M5.E12's `verified-against`
stamp; the process fix is cheaper — **check the date on any figure that supports your argument.**

**Don't let a self-attack section stand in for adversarial review.** §6 was authored by the audit, and
**§6.1 was the single largest source of REVIEW findings.** Self-attack is necessary and it is not
sufficient.

## What to feed back into Signal

**1 — Signal's tiers conflate "how careful must the thinking be" with "how much code-rigor ceremony
applies," and no tier says *strict thinking, zero ceremony*.** Flagged at DISCUSS as a deliberate
close-time question (D-M5E7-2) and it held all Epic. This Epic needed `gate_strictness: strict` +
`plan_validation_dims: all` + `context_rot_reread: true` with `tdd_required`, `security_audit`,
`performance_pass`, `simplification_pass`, `nyquist_enforcement` **all off** — and had to hand-tune an
Epic-scoped PROFILE to get it. **SPIKE was rejected twice on substance:** it skips SHIP (so the FR1
gate never fires, so `isEpicDone` stays false forever), and its retro template **has no
`## What to feed back into Signal`** — running the harvest-the-feedback Epic at SPIKE would have
produced the one retro shape that feeds nothing back. **Recommendation: document the hand-tune as a
named pattern (a "research-Epic" PROFILE recipe) rather than adding a fifth tier.** One data point
does not justify a tier; it does justify a reference.

**2 — `B41` (`confirmed`, P2, cataloged at REVIEW): four of seven phase commands never advance
`phase`.** `transitionPhase` is called only by `calibrate.md`, `discuss.md`, `ship.md`. In a
command-driven project a full run ends with `completed_phases: [DISCUSS]` and PLAN/EXECUTE/VERIFY/
REVIEW **never recorded**, while `/sig:status` reports `DISCUSS` throughout. `plan.md:50`'s
*"verify current phase is PLAN"* is **unsatisfiable by any command.** And because `plan`/`verify`/
`review` all call `markFresh`, each **stamps a fresh timestamp over the stale position** — turning
*stale-and-flagged* into **stale-and-silent**. Homed in M5.E9; deterministic and file-shaped, so it
needs no measurement layer.

**3 — A null-result check needs a date, not a trigger.** The roadmap's own falsifier —
*"the ports still never happen"* — **cannot fire on a trigger, because nothing fires on nothing.**
Signal's record has this failure twice already: the synthesizer-validator trigger closed **only
because someone deliberately went and checked**, and the GitHub-Issues trigger **fired 2026-07-15 and
promoted nothing across ≥2 drains.** REVIEW landed the falsifier as a dated check (2026-10-26) whose
default verdict on silence is *"the reframe was decorative."* **Generalize it: any watchlist entry
whose promote-back condition is an absence needs an expiry date, and `B39`'s fix should make a
checked-and-declined condition distinguishable from an unchecked one.**

**4 — Verification checks belong under the same scrutiny as the artifacts.** Two of this Epic's
mechanical checks false-failed (line-wrap, self-match). Cheap standing rules: **grep multi-line
prose with `-z` or a normalized buffer**, and **scope a self-referential check to the rows it
governs.** `B40` catalogues the adjacent class — citation integrity in `analysis/` is unenforceable
because the authoring convention routes around the one guard that could check it.

**5 — The headline finding is itself the feedback: Signal cannot detect whether its own interventions
work.** No test asserts a prompt instruction was obeyed, against a measured **7-of-12 adherence and a
7.12× output-token spread on byte-identical code.** M5.E8 is unconditional-next and gates every
prompt-shaped port. **Everything else in this list is downstream of it.**

## Anti-rationalization moment

**At REVIEW, the available shortcut was to ratify §6.** The roadmap ships an eight-subsection
*"What this roadmap gets wrong"* section that already names the confirmation-bias risk, the method's
blind spot, the survivorship bias, and the second-hand supply. It would have been easy — and would
have read as rigorous — to verify that §6 exists, note that the audit is admirably self-critical, and
return PASS.

**We didn't, and §6.1 turned out to be the single largest source of findings in the review.** Two of
its three self-defences do not answer the charge §6.1 itself states: **(c)** — *"the one capability
Brett pushed back on was reopened against the audit's conclusion"* — is evidence that **a human
corrected the audit**, which §6.2 concedes outright; an external catch is a *symptom* of the bias, not
a defence against it. **(a)** rebuts attachment to the going-in thesis, not the Signal-native
preference in the heading; cutting a **port group** confirms that shape rather than rebutting it.
**Withdrawn and relabelled respectively.** And the falsifier that carried the whole argument lived
only in `analysis/` — **the exact location D-M5E7-8(b) names as enforced by nothing**, written by the
same Epic that named it.

**The general lesson, and it is the one worth keeping:** *a self-attack authored by the party under
attack is the artifact most likely to defend while appearing to attack.* Ratifying it would have been
this phase's central failure mode, and the phase existed specifically to not do that.

*Second moment, smaller and during EXECUTE:* the AC2.3 grep returned a hit on item 24's *"factually
stale."* The available rationalization — **true on the merits** — was *"that's narrative, not a
rationale, so the check is a false positive."* It was reworded anyway, on the grounds that **a
mechanical check cannot tell narrative from rationale and neither can a fast reader.**

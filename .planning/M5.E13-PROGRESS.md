## ⛔ S3.t2 (`B46`) — stopped, because the requirement's premise does not survive measurement

**`B46` as written:** *"M5.E7's 45 dispositions were written to a side artifact and never stamped back into `ISSUES-INBOX.md`, so 47 candidates resurface identically at every future drain, `abandon` verdicts included."*

**Measured at EXECUTE, against both files:**

| Claim | Measured |
|---|---|
| 45 dispositions | **66 data rows** in `M5.E7-DISPOSITIONS.md` (89 pipe-lines − 10 headers − 13 separators). Verb column: 26 `continue`, 12 `abandon` (incl. 2 qualified), 11 `build`, 2 `split`, remainder summary/meta rows. |
| 47 inbox candidates | **48** (`listDrainCandidatesWithRecovery`, no dangling fence, 0 recovered) |
| The dispositions are verdicts **on** inbox entries | **No.** Token-overlap matching finds **0 of 48** inbox candidates corresponding to any disposition row. |

**What the dispositions actually are.** Their population is the **v2 feature-port candidates** — §3 is explicitly *"`MILESTONE-5.md` § 'Candidate v2 feature-port scope' — 23 items"*, plus the seed-§2 five (§4) and the Signal-native `N`-items (§5). All five of the file's references to the inbox cite it as **evidence of demand**, not as a target:

- `:156` — *"captured verbatim in `ISSUES-INBOX.md` (2026-07-26), **its single home**"*
- `:161` — *"Register **cluster I** — `ISSUES-INBOX.md:1475`, from a real external incident"*
- `:164` — *"Register **cluster E** … `ISSUES-INBOX.md`'s …"*

**Therefore the stamp-back described by FR2.2 would write verbs from one population onto a different one.** That is not a data correction; it is data corruption — and it is exactly the hazard M5.E9 cited when it refused a blanket defer (*"a blanket defer would have written wrong verbs over already-decided entries"*). The work is stopped rather than forced.

**This does not mean nothing is wrong.** 48 candidates genuinely do resurface at every drain, and that is a real cost. But its cause is not un-stamped dispositions — so the fix is not the one FR2.2 specifies, and choosing the real one is a scope call, not an implementation detail.

S3.t1 did exactly that — so at least one disposition **was** acted on, by an Epic that reached it through the bug ledger rather than through the disposition table. Which is its own small illustration of the class.

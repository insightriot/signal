## What changed mid-flight

**The dependency decision reversed.** PLAN's default was the manifest (`D-M6E1-3`), and `S3.t1` was
written as *"decide A-vs-B, default A."* It went the other way, on a number rather than a preference —
see below. `D-M6E1-7` supersedes `D-M6E1-3`, `FR3` was rewritten with its original ACs struck through
rather than deleted, `AC3.4` was **dissolved** (its precondition removed, not its requirement met),
and two new ACs were added: `AC3.6`, a mechanical trigger for reverting to the manifest, and `AC3.7`,
a faithfulness requirement for the vendored copy.

**`NFR3` went moot** the same way and is marked as such rather than quietly satisfied.

**Two ACs were reclassified as owed rather than met.** `AC4.4` and half of `AC5.1` need the
production deployment, which tracks `main` — structurally unreachable from a branch. Recorded as owed
at merge in PLAN, VERIFY and REVIEW rather than ticked in any of them.

## What assumptions broke

**1. `RESEARCH` §4's failure-mode sentence was inverted, and it was the deciding number.** It
described a failed dependency install as costing *"the 18 commands that never touch
`profile.js`/`state.js`"* — a minority tail, which is why `AC3.4` asked only that the outcome be made
**legible, not prevented**. Computed as a transitive closure instead of a grep for two filenames:
**28 of 51 `tools/lib` modules reach `yaml`, and 17 of 20 commands break without it.** `archive`,
`sweep`, `index` and `migrate-memory` are in the broken set and reach `yaml` through their own
modules — never through the two the grep named, which is exactly why it missed them.

So `AC3.4`'s subject was never a degraded mode. It was *"the plugin does not work, silently, at
import, on a restricted network."* **The AC did its job by being satisfied honestly: characterising
the outcome is what destroyed the option it was written to accompany.**

**2. `RESEARCH` rejected option C on a false premise.** Its stated ground was that named-export
interop is fragile when deep-importing a CJS package past its `exports` map. Verified in one command:
an `exports` map governs **bare specifiers only**, and a relative deep import yields both named
exports. C was viable and was declined on citation fidelity instead — the record now says the real
reason rather than inheriting the wrong one.

**3. The plan said "repoint 136 test imports." That was the smallest part of `S2`.** The work split
into five kinds the plan did not name, including nine maintainer scripts whose `./lib/` moved out
from under them, two shipped libraries with hardcoded paths, two dead `README.md` links, and
`state/` being **missing from the payload list entirely**.

## What surprised us

**The `.gitignore` exception nearly shipped a dependency containing nothing that runs.** Unanchored,
`dist/` also matched `plugin/node_modules/yaml/dist/` — the 796 KB that *is* the package. `git add -An`
reported `LICENSE`, `README.md` and `bin.mjs` and silently dropped the rest. It was caught by checking
the dry-run's file count against the tarball's own **233**, not by reading the pattern. **Reading the
rule would not have found it; counting the output did.**

**Three separate guards written during this Epic passed on nothing.**

1. The published-catalog tests passed two assertions against a file that did not exist —
   `JSON.stringify(null)` is `"null"`, which contains no sha and no `"ref":`.
2. `diffRequirementCoverage` reported `covered` on a denominator of **24 of 26** (`B100`).
3. The provenance test passed a mutation setting the version to `2.8.2`, twice — first because a
   heading elsewhere in the file satisfied it, then because the block names the version **twice** and
   `toContain` matched the other one.

**The pattern is one thing, and it is the Epic's real lesson: every one was found by reading the RED
run line-by-line or by mutating, and none by reading the assertion.** A guard that has never failed
has not been tested.

**A check cleared itself because the report mentioned it.** `diffRequirementCoverage` returned
`unattributableGroups: ["NFR-1".."NFR-4"]`; a bullet was added *disclosing* those four groups and the
value became `[]`. Nothing was verified in between. Reproduced deliberately: a report reading
*"NFR-1, NFR-2, NFR-3 and NFR-4 were not checked at all"* also returns `[]`. **A sentence declaring
groups unchecked satisfies the check whose job is to surface unchecked groups.** Filed as `B101`.

**A detector's own comment lied about it.** `detectP6LegacyMarketplaceSource` promised *"This never
throws"* while `join(homeDir, …)` sat outside the try. The test named *"never throws"* passed
throughout, because it supplied a valid `homeDir`.

## What we'd do differently

- **Compute reachability, never grep for two filenames.** The `S3` decision turned on a transitive
  closure that took one command to write. The inverted sentence had been in `RESEARCH` since PLAN and
  was read several times without being checked.
- **Mutate every guard that passes on its first run.** Three did here; all three were hollow. Make
  the rule explicit: a new assertion that is green on first execution is unverified until a planted
  violation reddens it.
- **Count the output of a mechanical step against an independent expectation.** The `.gitignore` trap
  was invisible in the pattern and obvious in the file count.
- **Name outcomes, never letter them.** `RESEARCH`'s **B** and `AC3.5`'s vendoring were different
  options, and `PLAN` S3.t1 cited both at once — the `M5.E17` class, costing a real disambiguation
  step mid-decision.
- **Write the provenance when vendoring, not at REVIEW.** `AC3.7` claimed the copy was *provable* for
  three slices while nothing in the repo proved it.

## What to feed back into Signal

- **`B100` and `B101` are the priority, and they are the same family.** One is a denominator that
  silently omits ids; the other an exclusion list that empties on mention. Both turn *"I could not
  look"* into a reassuring word (`covered`, `[]`). Both live in the tooling that checks Signal's own
  claim integrity, and **both were found by this Epic rather than by them.**
- **`AC0.1`'s semantic half now has field evidence, not an argument.** `B101` is *"everything here
  compares tokens"* reproduced on a live artifact, and worse than the abstract statement suggests,
  because the satisfying sentence can assert the opposite of coverage.
- **A `PASS-WITH-FIXES` should require mutation evidence for its in-phase coverage**, not merely a
  green test. This REVIEW's own fix shipped a hollow assertion twice before mutation caught it, and
  the rule currently asks only that new coverage be *"written in-phase and green."*
- **The adherence ceiling should be reported at phase close, not only when a test breaks.** This Epic
  added 5 directive lines to `doctor.md`, **all 5 unmeasurable**, and published coverage fell
  21.5% → 21.3%. That surfaced as a failing test, which is the right mechanism but the wrong moment.
- **`AC1.2b` earned its keep.** Written as a pre-emptive scope note about absolute user-config paths;
  `S6` then introduced exactly such a path. A requirement written to stop a future argument stopped it.

## Anti-rationalization moment

**The moment: `S3`'s decision was made, and the tempting move was not to re-check the number it
rested on.**

PLAN had a default (the manifest), `RESEARCH` had a recommendation (the manifest), `DECISIONS.md` had
`D-M6E1-3` recording it, and `AC3.5` only asked that vendoring be *weighed*. Weighing it against the
research as written would have taken one paragraph and produced *"A, as planned"* — three artifacts
agreeing, an AC discharged, and a slice closed in minutes.

The sentence that stopped it was `RESEARCH`'s own: *"the 18 commands that never touch
`profile.js`/`state.js` work and the rest fail."* It was oddly specific and easy to accept.
Recomputing it as a transitive closure took one command and returned **17 of 20 commands break** —
the inverse. **The whole `S3` decision, `D-M6E1-3`'s supersession, `FR3`'s rewrite and `AC3.4`'s
dissolution all follow from that one check**, which nothing required and which three prior readings
of the same paragraph had not prompted.

**The near-rationalization was not "skip the check." It was "the decision is already recorded, so the
number is settled."** A recorded decision is evidence that somebody decided, not that they were right,
and this repo's own `D-M5E19-6` — an Epic whose premise was false, proven by running it — had already
made that exact point five releases earlier.

**A second, smaller one:** at REVIEW the provenance test went green, and the honest option was to
accept it. Mutating it produced a pass; mutating it a second way produced another pass. Only the third
attempt failed. Stopping at the first green — or at the first mutation — would have shipped a guard
that could not fail.

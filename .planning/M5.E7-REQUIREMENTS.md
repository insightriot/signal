# M5.E7 — Requirements: the v2 direction audit (BR-8)

**Epic:** M5.E7 · **Tier:** FULL (Epic-scoped — `M5.E7-PROFILE.md`; ceremony off, gates strict)
**Decisions:** D-M5E7-1 … D-M5E7-5 (`DECISIONS.md`, 2026-07-25)
**Deliverable:** `analysis/SIGNAL-V2-ROADMAP.md` — a document. **No code ships in this Epic.**

---

## Objective

Decide what Signal should build next, and in what order, grounded in what Signal's own
record says it needs — with the nine inspiration repos as a **menu of available supply**
rather than a checklist of unabsorbed remainder (D-M5E7-1).

Output is a sequenced roadmap Brett **ratifies**, with an explicit **build / abandon /
continue** disposition on every candidate (D-M5E7-4). "Abandon" is a first-class outcome;
if nothing can be cut, the reframe was decorative.

**Success looks like:** the next Epic after M5.E7 can be opened straight from the roadmap
without re-litigating what to build or why, and every port candidate that existed before
this Epic has a recorded verb against it.

## Functional Requirements

### FR1 — Harvest Signal's own demand record

Mine the first-party record into a deduplicated **demand register**, each item citing its
source file and section.

Sources, in priority order:

| Source | What to extract | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| `*-RETROSPECTIVE.md` | `## What to feed back into Signal` (16 files), `## What assumptions broke` (17), `## What surprised us` (17), `## What we'd do differently` (16) | 18 files on disk; 17 Epic-ID-shaped |
| `BUGS.md` | Open items **and patterns across closed ones** | B32–B36 open; B1–B31 closed |
| `BACKLOG.md` | Sequenced items not yet promoted | — |
| `ISSUES-INBOX.md` | Raw un-triaged capture | — |
| `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` | Unresolved design questions | 2 open |

**AC1.1** — Every retro carrying `## What to feed back into Signal` is read; extracted items
land in the register with a `{file}#{section}` citation.
**AC1.2** — The register is **deduplicated by theme**, not by ticket. Carry-over chains are
collapsed into single signals: **B24 → B27/B28 → B34/B35 is one recurring signal about the
migrate dangling-gate's fail-safe posture, not three tickets.**
**AC1.3** — Each register entry records **frequency and recency** (how many Epics raised it,
how recently) — a thing raised in five retros outranks a thing raised once.
**AC1.4** — The **two retros lacking** the feedback section (`M4.5.E4`, `M4.5.E8`) are noted
as gaps, not silently skipped.
**AC1.5 — stub-retro gap is reported, not backfilled.** Four of the 17 enumerated retros are
stubs (`M4.5.E1`, `M4.5.E3`, `M4.5.E6`, `M4.5.E7`). The audit records **what those Epics were
and what signal is therefore missing**, and does **not** attempt reconstruction.
*Rationale — this is a deliberate non-goal:* a retro's value is that it was written when the
pain was fresh. Reconstructing one months later produces plausible narrative, not evidence,
and would contaminate the register with invented demand. The stubs are a **finding about
Signal's retro discipline**, and belong in `## What to feed back into Signal` at close.

| Repo | Depth | Why |
|---|---|---|

**AC2.1** — Each repo's entry records what was checked, against which version/commit, and on
what date — so the next audit knows what this one actually saw.
**AC2.2** — Any **material divergence** from the April characterization is called out
explicitly (capability added, removed, abandoned, or superseded upstream).
**AC2.3** — A repo that has gone **dormant or been abandoned upstream** is flagged as such —
that alone can justify an `abandon` disposition.
**AC2.4** — Supply is recorded **independently of demand** before matching. No candidate is
pre-judged by whether FR1 found a want for it.

## Non-Functional Requirements

These are the NFRs that actually bind a **document** deliverable.

**NFR1 — Traceability.** Every demand claim cites `{file}#{section}`; every supply claim cites
the repo and what was inspected. A reader must be able to check any claim without re-running
the audit.
**NFR2 — Faithfulness / no silent drops.** Nothing in the pre-existing candidate scope
disappears without a recorded verb. AC3.2's five-item list is the hard floor.
**NFR3 — Bounded scope.** The reframe widens the input surface from 9 repos to "everything
Signal has recorded" — a real over-run risk. Mining is depth-limited; the no-silent-drops floor
is the completeness contract, not exhaustive trawling. **If the audit starts generating new
work items rather than dispositioning existing ones, it has slipped scope.**
**NFR4 — Reproducibility.** The roadmap's method section must let a later audit re-run this one
and diff the result.
**NFR5 — Reversibility.** Superseded docs are relocated with pointers, never deleted.
**NFR6 — Honest uncertainty.** Where evidence is thin — notably the total absence of written
tester feedback (D-M5E7-3) — the roadmap says so rather than over-claiming.

### Tier-aware NFR checklist (FULL) — explicitly dispositioned

The FULL-tier prompt asks about health/liveness probes, graceful shutdown, structured request
logging, security headers, and rate limiting. **All five are N/A:** the deliverable is a
markdown document with no runtime, no process, no request path, and no network surface. Recorded
as N/A rather than skipped, per the FULL NFR discipline — and consistent with D-M5E7-2, which
turned the code-ceremony flags off precisely so they would not be waved away ad hoc at each gate.

## Out of Scope (Not Doing)

- **Building any port.** M5.E7 decides; the port Epics (M5.E8+) build. A finding of "build X"
  does not authorize starting X inside this Epic.
- **Backfilling the four stub retros** — AC1.5; reconstruction would manufacture evidence.
- **Re-opening the per-repo compounding substrate** — settled 2026-07-15 (seed §3).
- **Recruiting testers or running a usage study.** Real usage data would improve sequencing, but
  gathering it is a separate effort and BR-8 has waited on the doc-runtime long enough. The
  roadmap flags where such data would have changed a call.
- **Changing Signal's tier system** to accommodate research Epics. The gap is real (D-M5E7-2)
  but it is a `## What to feed back into Signal` finding, not this Epic's work.

## Open Questions

1. **Does the 10-phase architecture survive the reframe?** `SIGNAL-INTEGRATION-RUNDOWN.md`'s
   10-phase target descends from the absorb-the-repos frame. Under D-M5E7-1 it is a hypothesis
   to test, not a given — the audit may conclude Signal's real shape is 7 phases plus a strong
   doc-runtime. AC5.4 forces an answer.
2. **Do the eight self-invented commands imply a pattern worth naming?** `init`/`status`/
   `resume`/`add`/`checkpoint`/`doctor`/`index`/`migrate-memory`/`sweep` all serve *operating*
   a long-lived project rather than executing a phase. If that is a coherent product direction,
   it competes with the ports for the next Epic — and no source repo would have surfaced it.

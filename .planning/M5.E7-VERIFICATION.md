## §2 — What the verification run found

Three items. **Two were defects in the checks themselves** — worth recording, because a check that
false-fails is as dangerous as one that false-passes, and this Epic catalogued that exact class
(`B40`).

**§2.1 — Two mechanical checks were wrong before the artifacts were.**
- **AC7.3 returned 0 and the criterion was satisfied.** The caveat is present at §6.3; the grep
  failed because the sentence **wraps across a line** (`written up\nfront`). A naive single-line
  grep on prose is a **false negative**, and I was one step from recording a FAIL on a passing
  criterion. Re-run wrap-tolerant (`tr '\n' ' '`) → **1 hit**.
- **AC2.3 returned 1 hit and was clean.** The forbidden-word grep matched **the audit row that
  names the forbidden words** in order to describe the check. Scoped to disposition rows → **0**.
  *(Both are recorded in `M5.E7-DISPOSITIONS.md` §6 as well.)*

**§2.2 — AC3.1's naive row-count over-counted by five.** `grep -c` over row-shaped lines returned
**50** because §8.1 **re-lists items 1–6** as amendments rather than as new candidates. Enumerating
**distinct IDs** gives the true figure: **45**. *The lesson is the Epic's own: a count derived from a
pattern is not a count derived from the thing.*

**§2.3 — One real gap, found and fixed.** `BACKLOG.md` carried **12 work items but only 11 were
discoverable from the M5.E8–E12 block** — the 12th (**retro replay**) was sequenced into M5.E9 while
living in its Sprint-4 home, so a reader of the Epic block would never see it. **Fixed** with a
pointer under M5.E9 (a pointer, **not** a copy — single-home, per the rule `B37` exists to protect),
plus a first slice and done-when added at its Sprint-4 home. *This is precisely the
"easy to navigate for the agents" failure M5.E12 is scoped to address, found inside Signal's own
backlog on the day it was written.*

## §4 — What this verification cannot establish

Stated because `M5.E7-VALIDATION.md` D4 flagged it before the Epic began, and it remains true:

> *"The mechanical checks verify that each candidate **got** a verb — they cannot verify the verb
> was **right**. Nothing in this plan can."*

**Every check above confirms the audit is well-formed. None confirms it is correct.** Specifically
unverifiable by any check in §1:

1. **Whether the 15 cuts are the right cuts.** AC2.3 proves no cut cites staleness; it cannot prove
   the fit/overlap arguments are sound.
2. **Whether the reframe is real or self-serving.** 10 of 11 builds are Signal-native — the shape a
   confirmation-biased audit would produce. Roadmap §6.1 carries the self-attack **and a falsifier**
   (*if M5.E8 lands, measurement shows Signal's enforcement is as good as claimed, and the ports
   still never happen, the reframe was decorative*). **A falsifier is not a defence** — it is a
   commitment to find out later.
3. **Whether the sequence is right.** Only that every item has a trigger — and **`B39` means no
   trigger is currently evaluated by anything.**
4. **The upstream cuts rest on positioning, not evidence** (AC7.3). The corpus is structurally blind
   there; the roadmap says so in §6.3 rather than dressing a judgment as data.

**All four route to REVIEW**, repurposed per AC7.4 to attack the *reasoning*. **This is where the
Epic fails if it fails.** Point the panel at roadmap §6 and dispositions §6 — the self-attacks are
the most load-bearing and least externally-checked claims in the Epic.

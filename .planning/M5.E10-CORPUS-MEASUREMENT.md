## FR9 is the narrowest check the Epic shipped, and it cannot see the case that produced it

**1 of 12, and the one is Signal's own repository.** Eight corpus projects keep no `BACKLOG.md`; the
three that do carry rows naming nothing a machine can resolve to a closure record — their headings
are prose titles, and their `BUGS.md` files (where they have one) carry no ids either.

That includes **`eval-project-A`, the project `B94` was filed from** — a backlog two weeks stale with
roughly fifteen shipped slices reading as pending. The check reports `cannot-evaluate` there, which
is correct and is also the point: **it does not detect the defect that motivated it.** What it can do
for that project is the `dischargeBacklogRows` half — the closing mechanism that did not exist at all
— which is unaffected by whether the *detector* can link a row to evidence.

Left as measured rather than fixed by loosening the rule. The only widenings available are prose
matching between a row's title and a shipped unit's title, which manufactures findings (`FR8`
rejected exactly that when a literal reading produced 62 episodes against 5 real ones), or treating
row age as closure, which is a guess wearing a number.

## The headline is the flagship check, and it is not flattering

**FR1 — the requirement-coverage diff, this Epic's headline deliverable — can evaluate 2 of 12
projects.** Not "found nothing wrong in 10". **Could not look at 10.**

The reason is structural, and it is the same fact that produced `D-M5E10-6`: ten of twelve projects
have **no REQUIREMENTS artifact scoped to the same unit as their VERIFICATION**. Some keep one
project-level `REQUIREMENTS.md` covering every phase; most keep none at all and let the PLAN carry
the criteria. The check is not broken on those projects — **it is inapplicable**, and it says so
rather than returning a clean result.

`eval-project-C` is the instructive case: it reports `coverage:n` and it is the project the AC1.5
fixture came from. Its requirements are real, thorough, and project-scoped — which is precisely why
the naive pairing this check refuses would have produced a red wall.

## What this means for the Epic's claims

**Do not read "this Epic shipped seven checks" as "seven checks now run on your projects".** Written
honestly:

- **One check works everywhere** — FR4's correction protocol, 12/12, because it needs nothing but
  readable markdown.
- **One works nearly everywhere** — FR3's template gate, 10/12, because it needs a tier and an
  artifact.
- **Five are minority-shape checks** — FR1, FR2, FR7, FR8 and FR9 apply to between **1 and 4** of
  twelve, because they need document conventions most projects do not have: unit-scoped requirements,
  a Nyquist map in a comparable namespace, retrospectives on disk, Epic mode, a backlog whose rows
  name their work.

**Signal's own repository is in the minority on every one of those five — and on FR9 it is the
entire majority.** That is the `M5.E16`
lesson repeating: the shape this repo has is not the shape the corpus has, and a check validated only
here is validated against an unrepresentative sample.

## What would raise the numbers, and what would not

**Not:** loosening the checks. Every "could not evaluate" above is a case where guessing would
produce a confident wrong answer — pairing a project-scoped REQUIREMENTS against a unit-scoped
VERIFICATION reports every other unit's requirements as missing, and aligning two id namespaces that
merely look similar invents contradictions. Those are the failures the three-outcome rule exists to
prevent, and `M5.E10`'s own build produced both of them before the rules were tightened.

**Yes:** the conventions themselves. FR1 becomes evaluable on a project the moment it writes
requirements scoped to the unit being verified. The check is a reason to adopt the convention, not a
verdict on projects that have not.

## What this measurement itself could not establish

- **It measures applicability, not correctness.** A check counted "evaluable" on a project was not
  audited for whether its answer there is *right*. Twelve green cells for FR4 mean twelve projects
  are searchable, not that twelve corpora are free of uncorrected claims.
- **It reads only `.planning/`.** A project keeping requirements in an issue tracker, a wiki, or a
  README is counted blind, which is accurate for these checks and says nothing about the project.
- **Twelve projects is the corpus, not a sample of software.** All twelve belong to one maintainer
  and share habits. The conventions they lack, they lack together.

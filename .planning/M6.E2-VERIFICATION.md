## What this could not establish

- **Whether `bug-status-vs-changelog` is precise enough to be worth running.** Measured on this
  repository it flags 2 rows of which 1 is real. That is one repository and one point in time. Its
  precision on any other project is unmeasured, and the rule cannot improve without semantic
  comparison, which is out of scope by decision (`D-M6E2-7`).
- **Whether the reach figures are still true.** They were measured on 2026-08-18 across 12 local
  projects. Nothing re-measures them in CI — measuring needs the corpus, which CI does not have. The
  figures are single-homed, not self-verifying, and the code says so.
- **Whether `facts-attribution` catches a wrong number.** It cannot. It detects only that figures are
  attributed to a release other than the installed one; deriving a true test count means running the
  suite. The check's own finding text says this.
- **Anything about non-Signal projects beyond file shapes.** The corpus is 12 local projects with one
  maintainer, and the probe counts the presence of a convention, not whether the convention is
  correct in those projects.
- **Whether five new checks degrade `/sig:resume` in practice.** `AC5.3` is verified structurally —
  not-applicable checks render as a count, the reach caveat is one grouped line — but no reader
  tolerance was measured, and none is claimed.
- **The `[Unreleased]` and `facts.md` findings are real and deliberately unfixed.** The first needs a
  product decision (`OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`); the second is left for `cut-release.js` at SHIP when the
  test count is final. Both are open at the time of this report, by choice, and would otherwise read
  as verification failures.
- **No independent reading.** Research, planning and verification all ran single-context (subagent
  dispatch disabled this session). REVIEW is the first place a second perspective enters, and this
  Epic's history says that matters: two of its defects were caught only by running a tool against the
  work rather than by reading it.
